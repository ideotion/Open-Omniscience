## 3. Cross-surface patterns

1. **The offline coachmark (`#net-coach`) is a shared app-shell liability, and its severity scales with viewport, not with content.** Confirmed overlapping real controls on law, indices, analyze, and custody; on Law at 375px it crossed from cosmetic to a genuine 30-second Playwright actionability timeout. It also outlives the surface it opened on (still covering Markets chrome after a user leaves Analyze). This is one fix, in one component, that resolves four (soon likely more) filed findings.

2. **i18n gaps share one shape everywhere they were checked: an untranslated string sitting directly beside a correctly-translated sibling on the *same* grid or sentence**, not a wholesale-untranslated screen. Library (Overview stat labels), Indices (provenance note, 8/9 tag chips), and Help (doc-nav chrome, critic-found) all reproduce this exact pattern. Library additionally surfaced a punctuation-joined bidi corruption (date ranges reordering visually in RTL) that the brief itself flags as a known trap — worth a dedicated regex/audit pass for other punctuation-joined values (versions, IDs, URLs, ranges) app-wide. **Analyze — the surface the project's whole honesty-by-construction pitch rests on — received zero non-English testing this round**, and given the hit rate on every other tested surface, that is the single highest-value locale gap to close next.

3. **"Controls generated in a loop over a large dataset never get their accessible-name/structure revisited."** Settings' 3618-row source table (label/select-name, critical), Settings' 241-row calendar directory (nested-interactive), and Analyze's tag-pill switcher (nested-interactive) are three independent instances of the same authoring habit — a hand-written row template that was correct for one row and never re-audited at scale.

4. **The shared contrast-measurement harness (`oohelp.py`) was mid-patch during this exact audit window**, and its bug (excluding an element's own painted background from the composited backdrop, scoring self-filled buttons/pills against the *panel* behind them instead) was independently rediscovered by four separate agents (law, indices, settings, custody) — all four also independently found it already fixed by the time their verify pass ran (source comment: *"CORRECTED 2026-09-08... Two audit agents caught this independently"*). This is a real process cost: four expensive investigations converged on one already-known, already-fixed tooling bug. **Recommendation for the next round: pin the harness commit for the duration of a single audit run**, so "the same measurement changed answers between a walk and its own verify pass" stops happening.

5. **Diagnosis-before-mechanism traps recurred twice, both caused by native browser dialogs being invisible to screenshots and silently auto-dismissed by unconfigured automation.** Law's "dead link" (actually a `confirm()` no test had a `page.on('dialog',...)` handler for) and Custody's OTS consent flow both needed exactly the same workaround (a registered dialog handler) to see what was really happening. Any future click-driven audit on this app should register a dialog handler by default, not opportunistically.

6. **Two "this feature doesn't render because the fixture has no data" findings were mis-scoped in their original framing and corrected by verify/critic in opposite directions:** Indices' idx-01 over-claimed scope (folding in an unused, always-empty `commodity` API catalog that the live UI never even calls, when a real, populated Commodities tab sits one click away and does correctly exercise invariant #16). Custody's under-tested success path (P2-11) is the mirror case — genuinely nothing in any available fixture exercises it, and no sibling surface with real data was available to substitute.

7. **Coverage assignment itself is a finding.** Three of eight assigned surfaces (custody, integrity, help) arrived with no data or partial data; the completeness critic's spot-check found the single most serious issue in this entire round (the popstate/TOC ejection bug) purely because it happened to test hash-navigation, a class no other surface's agents were tasked to try.

## 5. Visual impression of these surfaces as a set

Across all six fully-driven surfaces, the visual language is consistent and disciplined: dark "ink" theme by default, a shared amber caveat color, uniform card/panel framing, and — where data exists — genuinely dense, well-labelled tables that never fake a number. The strongest screens in this round (Library's Composition/Storage, Law's Groups "computed" lens, Analyze's Overview/Sentiment/Competitive) are the ones where the honesty-by-construction discipline and the visual design reinforce each other rather than compete. The two recurring visual failures are structural, not stylistic: the offline coachmark colliding with real chrome (four surfaces), and a light/paper theme whose contrast math was evidently never re-verified after invariant #23's original ink-theme-focused fix (Library). Where a surface is genuinely empty of real data (Indices' board, several Law/Statistics selects), the emptiness is disclosed honestly rather than looking broken — the app degrades loudly, as designed. Help stands apart from this pattern entirely: it is the one surface in this round that is visually and functionally broken at mobile width, and it carries a defect (silent ejection to Home) that undermines trust in the app's own reference documentation in a way none of the data-surface findings do.

## 6. Complexity & automation notes — Settings and Help

**Settings** is the app's largest surface by a wide margin: 146 of 2,492 discoverable controls were exercised this round, and the DOM grows from 6,954 nodes (settled Graphics) to 19,257 once all 10 Advanced accordions are opened — and even that figure was initially undercounted by ~5.7x for buttons specifically, due to an async-load race in the audit's own instrumentation (§2 IDEA note). A first-time user needs on the order of 4–6 controls (theme, language, density, default result count, maybe accent); everything else — a 3,618-row source table, a 241-row calendar directory, ~30 numeric "safe range" tunables, and ~20 standalone AI/diagnostic benchmark buttons — is power-user, developer, or at-risk-journalist tooling, uniformly and honestly subtitled but given **equal visual weight** to "Density" in a flat accordion list, up to and including a "Bulletin" report generator nested inside "Uninstall & wipe" (P3-7). Both real accessibility defects found here (P1-9, P2-7) live exactly where automation generates one row of controls per dataset entry and the accessible-name/structure was written once and never revisited at scale — that is the highest-leverage place to look for the *next* instance of this defect class, before it's found the hard way again. Concrete automation candidates the walk itself surfaced: default-and-hide the ~30 numeric tunables behind the existing "Reset to defaults" pattern (Appearance already does this), and group the ~20 diagnostic buttons by audience (everyday/power-user/developer) instead of one flat "Diagnostics" heading.

**Help** is smaller by control count but came out of this round as the highest-severity single surface, entirely because a completeness critic tested a class of interaction (hash/anchor navigation) that no other surface's agents were tasked with. Its markdown renderer and its hand-written Table of Contents were evidently built independently and never round-tripped against each other — the renderer emits headings with no `id`, the TOC links to anchors that therefore don't exist, and the app's own global `popstate` handler's Home-fallback silently converts every one of those broken clicks into full navigation away from the page the user was reading. Only one TOC link (`#30-home`) was actually clicked; the manual has at minimum eight sibling documents (Quickstart, Ethics, Governance, Security, Design, Roadmap, Architecture, Contributing, Changelog) whose TOC-anchor pairing was never checked and is presumed to share the same defect. Combined with Help's mobile breakage (the only surface in this round that fails to reflow at 375px) and its RTL-untranslated doc-nav chrome, Help should be treated as **needing a full walk+verify pass at the same depth as the other six**, not the spot-check it received.
