## 2026-10-17 — A long hold's cause is its split by kind of work, and the part that gives way to the zip's budget is the shortest holds' (PR #1421)

**What was found.** The 16 Oct bundles held the gate for up to 685 s. The slow-query ring said which STATEMENTS were slow (INSERT INTO articles 58.6 %, SAVEPOINT 11.5 %, a mention-row read inside the hold about 16 %), the journal said the phase of a SAMPLE (pool-wait, execute, commit, python), and neither said how the HOLD was spent. The gate now keeps, for the hold in progress, the seconds its owner thread spent in INSERT statements, savepoint statements, any other statement and commits, and what no measured statement covers is `between_statements`; a hold of 30 s or more writes the split as `seconds_by`, as the watchdog's sample (a statement still running counted so far) and exactly at its end.

**The rules.**
1. **Count in the listeners that already see every statement, and make a non-owner return at once.** `_owner != get_ident()` is one attribute read: measured on `SELECT 1` +1 us a statement for every other thread, +4.5 us for the holder. The ledger is written by the owner thread only, so it needs no lock; a new hold is told from an old one by the grant counter, so a hold without a statement costs nothing.
2. **A statement that raises has no after-event.** `handle_error` ends its seconds where it failed; without it the failed statement keeps counting to the next work. A session's commit has an exact end (`after_commit`); a raw connection's has none, so it is an upper bound closed at the next work or the release, and the test says so.
3. **A sample of a running hold counts the statement in flight**, or a hold that never ends (the process was killed inside an INSERT) shows the split of everything but its last statement.
4. **The budget is not raised; the member cuts, and the count is computed.** With every name at the cut the journal is over its 125,000-byte row by the splits (126,163 with all twenty), so the member takes the split off the shortest holds first until it fits, writes `zip-budget` in their place and counts them (6 of 20 keep it at the widest, all 20 at ordinary widths). The test proves it fits, that the holds keeping it are the longest, that it is MAXIMAL (putting one more back overflows) and that the journal file keeps every split.
5. **A missing split is a reason, never zeros.** A hold written by a build without the field says `not-recorded`.
