## 2026-10-27 — a figure cited as a reason names its statement, its size and the knob that sets its magnitude, and a copy of a measured figure carries the label of the original

**What was found.** #1543 retracted one such figure (`fts.py:844`, "74 s on a 2.7 GB encrypted corpus": what it timed was not recorded). The premise sweep (WAL lane, 2026-10-27; the list is `premise-sweep-2026-10-27.md` in the project's shared folder `wal-lane/`, not in the repo) went looking for the rest of the class: 456 paragraphs in `src/` cite a duration beside a measurement word, 135 name at most one of statement, size, store or temperature, and twelve were re-timed on a 4-core box as ratios or growth shapes (the box is 1.3 to 1.6 times slower than the field boxes on CPU work, so seconds are not compared). Nine reproduced (the libyaml loader ratios on three files, the 50,000-city gazetteer load, both quadratic regex cliffs, the 159 ms key derivation, the eight-thread config build, the kernel thread clock against `/proc`), two reproduced in direction with a different magnitude and one did not. Those three, and one label, are dated here so that the next reader of the cite finds the correction beside it. Nothing in the cited files was edited; no decision changes.

- **`src/ingest/lock_stats.py:8`, "a 10 MiB nav-heavy page measured 85 s".** 2026-10-27: 27.9 s at 10 MiB on a generated flat navigation page through `extract_article` (1.37, 2.68 and 5.07 s at 0.5, 1 and 2 MiB, linear). The cited page shape is not recorded, so 85 s is not reproduced; the reason it is cited for (one long hold stalls every other extraction) holds at 28 s.
- **`src/database/writer.py:244`, the FIFO gate against the barging one.** 2026-10-27, 4 cores, 50 workers x 40 holds x 200 microseconds, median of 5: the pre-FIFO gate (git `e2a2a9871^`) 456 ms wall with a worst wait of 223 ms; the shipped gate 614 ms (+35%) with a worst wait of 18.3 ms (12x better); a shared-condition `notify_all` FIFO 1,869 ms. The comment cites +16% wall and a 31x worst-wait gain. The sign of the trade and the herd's cost reproduce; the magnitudes differ, and holds per worker, which decides them, was never recorded. The decision (a `max_wait_s` that measures starvation cannot be read) stands.
- **`src/monitoring/merge_diag.py:50`, "parsing all of it costs ~90 s of CPU".** 2026-10-27: 8.7 million generated `merge_statement_begin` records, 1.58 GB (182 B each, the cited journal's 184): the whole-file `_attribute` parse took 47.8 s of CPU on this box (the field box is about 1.9x slower, the same order as 90 s) and **peaked at 5.0 GB resident, 3x the file**; the shipped head+tail read (64 MiB) took 1.6 s. The resident size, which the cite does not mention, is the stronger reason for the head+tail bound.
- **The 50 and 100 million document sharding costing** (`docs/ROADMAP.md:269` and the OPEN_QUEUE "OPERATOR JOB" entry: "MEASURED here: ... so 89.6 GiB and 5.2 h per arm at 50 M"). The rows for 50 M and 100 M are linear **[EXTRAPOLATION]** from one run of `fts_calibrate.py` over 300,000 synthetic documents (180 to 420 words, Zipf vocabulary, contentless-delete, hashsize 64 MiB, page size 16384, 4 cores), as `docs/design/STORAGE_5TB_REFRESH_2026-09-07.md` §5.1 labels them; the ledger and roadmap copies carry neither the setup nor the label. The arithmetic re-checks (1,925 B x 50 M = 89.64 GiB; 50 M / 2,692 docs/s = 5.16 h; 179.28 GiB and 10.32 h at 100 M). Read those figures as a ceiling extrapolated from 300,000 documents, not as a measurement at 50 M.

**The rules.**

1. **A figure cited as a reason records the statement or function, the size, the machine, and the knob that sets its magnitude.** The gate comment recorded workers and hold time and not holds per worker, which is the term that moved its two numbers by a factor of two to three; a "nav-heavy page" with no shape is unrepeatable. A cite that records all four can be re-run in a minute; one that records fewer can only be believed or retracted.
2. **Re-time a cited figure as a ratio or a growth shape before building on it, and say what the box was.** When the machine differs, the comparison that survives is the ratio between two loaders, the exponent of a cliff, or the sign of a trade, never the seconds; the nine that reproduced all did so in that sense.
3. **When a bound is cited for its time, measure its memory too.** The journal's head+tail read was justified by 90 s; the whole parse also needed 5.0 GB resident, which is the reason that would have survived a faster machine.
4. **A copy of a measured figure carries the label of the original.** The design note labelled its 50 M row an extrapolation; two later copies kept the number and dropped the label, and a reader of the copy cannot tell a measurement from a multiplication.
