## 2026-10-17 — an order scan is as blind as its list of what takes the resource: a session takes its connection lazily, gives it back at every commit and takes it again at the next loop turn (PR #1421)

**What was found.** The scan for "connection first, then the gate" looked for `engine.connect()`, `raw_connection()`, `begin()`, `session.connection()` and `begin_nested()` after the gate. The read of the final head found the same stall through a path none of those words name: `with write_lock(): session.get(...)` on a fresh session, or on one that had just committed or rolled back, checks the pooled connection out UNDER the gate (a session takes its connection when its first statement runs and gives it back at every commit, rollback and close). Twenty functions in three shapes: the first statement under the gate (`bulk_build`, `counter_deferral`, `corpus_epoch`, `fold_page`, the keyword writers), a rollback before the gate (`source_topics`), and the next turn of a loop after a commit (`email.delete_imported_newsletters`, the reconcile sweeps). `prune_orphan_keywords` held the gate across a slice's delete chunks and each chunk's commit gave the connection back, so every chunk after the first took the pool under the gate. With the pool's connections out (23 of 24 in the 16 Oct bundles) the gate holder stood for the pool timeout with the write queue behind it; the probe reproduced it with a pool of one.

**The rules.**
1. **When a scan proves an order, list the takers that take LAZILY.** Here: any use of a session (`execute`, `get`, `query`, `commit`, `flush`, `bulk_*`), not only the calls that say "connection". The scan names a session by its receiver (`session`, `db`, `sess`, `*_session`, a name bound from a session factory), and a raw connection (`conn`, `cur`) is not one: it holds its check-out already.
2. **A check-out covers nothing after the session gave it back.** A `connection_first` before a `commit()`/`rollback()`/`close()` of the same session, or before the loop whose body commits, is not a check-out for the gate that follows: the scan reads the loop body and the calls in between.
3. **An earlier read is not a check-out.** The read helpers may release the connection (`release_idle_connection`), so the guard is the explicit `connection_first(session)`, which is free when the session already holds one.
4. **Where a unit holds the gate across commits, the order cannot be kept: split the hold per commit and ask again, under the gate, whatever was decided before it.** The orphan prune now holds the gate per chunk and each chunk re-reads which of its keywords gained a mention (those stay, with their tags); the ids were decided outside the gate and a writer can now run between two chunks.
5. **A probe that reads the FIRST ask of the gate is satisfied by the seeding step.** A site that seeds its rows through a session of its own asks the gate first with a connection out; assert EVERY first ask, and make the sweeps cross a slice (one keyword per slice) so the second slice, after a commit, is the one asked.
6. **State what the scan still cannot see:** a checkout made deeper in a call under the gate (`stream_backup._drain_wal` -> `_wal_row` -> `engine.connect()`, F10).
