## 2026-10-18 — A hill-climb that waits for a count and needs a margin is slow where the best count is high, and a lab of uniform sources cannot show it

**What was found.** The maximum-mode governor (``src/scheduler/bandwidth.py``) now reads the articles STORED per window (R180) and takes one decision when a window has run 240 s and seen 100 source completions. Two things about that were NOT visible in the lab and were found by driving the real class with a fake clock against made-up plants (``osm-lane/governor-plan/noise_sim.py``, a simulation and not a measurement):
- The window's clock is the completion rate. At the lab's 25 completions a minute a window is 4 minutes; on a machine that completes 6 a minute (the simulated slow plant at 6 permits) it is 17 minutes, so the climb is one permit per quarter hour. In 3 simulated hours a plant whose best count is 30 permits reached a mean of 6 (21 % of its best rate), where 50 permits (the shipped governor's opening count) would have run at about 76 %. A plant whose best count is 12 reached a mean of 8 (67 %).
- A strict margin stops the climb where one more permit adds less than the margin: at 10 permits a further permit adds at most 10 % of the rate even on a plant that is still rising, so the rule holds there. Margins of 3 % and 5 % did not change the slow plant's result (the clock, not the margin, was the limit); a larger step after a "proven" rise made the lab's own plant worse (mean 22 to 40 permits against a best of 4 to 8) because a noisy window looks like a proven rise.

**The rules.**
1. **A rule that reads a count per window has a clock that scales with the count; say how many decisions a pass of a given speed gets.** The governor's reason names a window that is long enough but not full (``thin``); a pass of fewer than 100 completions never gets a decision and stays at its start of 4.
2. **The lab's best count is a property of the lab.** Every lab source returns the same number of articles, so the lab cannot measure the counting noise the completions floor protects against, and its flat curve (45 art/min at 1 permit, 58 from 4 to 8, 44.7 at 27) does not contain a latency-bound machine. A gate passed in that lab says the rule does not lose THERE.
3. **A cross-pass memory is the cheap thing that would help and was NOT built.** The governor is created per pass at its start; what one pass learned is lost, so a machine that needs 20 permits relearns the climb every pass. Persisting the count a pass settled at is a state-file change and a ruling, not a tuning.

**A second, unrelated fact from the same cells: the pinned WAL costs about 3.4 MB per article stored at the lab shape.** The lab's ``--pin`` keeps the WAL from being checkpointed; 2 passes of 1,200 articles peaked at 8,219 MB, 2 of 900 at 7,516 to 7,600 MB. A cell's disk need is the template copy (6.1 GB) plus about 3.4 MB for every article written in ALL its passes, including the warm-up pass the harness discards. The first G5 attempt ignored the second term, ran 236 ``No space left on device`` errors and stored 1,089 of 1,200; it was kept as invalid. The G5 script now refuses to start a cell below 14 GB free.
