## 2026-10-26 — a wall-clock limit is measured from the event it follows, and a red that cannot say how long it took cannot be told from a defect

`tests/test_connecting_allocation_failures.py::test_a_memory_error_at_any_instruction_of_the_cleanup_leaves_no_level_raised_except_at_the_release_call` failed once on one pull request's CI (Python 3.13.16, a `test` job of 1 h 19 min) with `release_shared_since:209 CALL: a swap that was already waiting for the open did not get through`, and passed on the same interpreter on three other runs. It is the sweep that raises `MemoryError` before each allocating instruction of `connecting()`'s cleanup and asks whether a swap that was already waiting for the open still got through; line 209 is `notify_all`, the one call after the level is given back, so the swap is woken by its own wait step and not by the notify.

**What the failure was.** The library was right: through-time at that site, measured from the swap thread's start, is 0.05 to 0.11 s, idle and beside 16 busy processes. The test measured it from the START OF THE SWAP'S THREAD, which includes the body's own 0.05 s wait and anything that delays the thread or the process before the cleanup begins, and held it to 0.5 s. A pause of 0.7 s in the body (the test's `Event().wait` stretched in a scratch copy, the library untouched) reproduces the CI text exactly, at that one site (the only site where the swap is woken by a step), and the old test goes red while the library is the same. What paused the CI process is NOT established: a full collection in a very large process, or runner steal, would each do it, and neither was observed.

**The rule.** Measure a limit from the later of the events it depends on ("the cleanup has begun" and "the swap is asking"), not from where a thread started; make the limit wide enough that a pause before that point cannot use it up (the swap's own deadline is 3.0 s, from when it asks, and the through-limit 1.0 s, about ten times the healthy figure and well under the deadline, so a lost wake-up with no wait step, which takes the whole deadline and is refused, is still told apart: the mutant that removes the step fails by name); and put the measured figure in the failure text ("it took 0.05 s; the limit is 1.0 s" against "was refused at its deadline"), so the next red says whether the process paused or the wake-up was lost. A test that reports only "did not get through" turns a stall and a defect into the same line, and the person reading it has to guess. A swap that cannot get through because a level is still raised is not waited for (its thread ends at its own deadline), which keeps the file's run at about 5 s.
