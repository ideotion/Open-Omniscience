## 2026-11-14 — a slow CI leg on a balanced deal is a slow runner; the wall left is runners waiting for a slot (OPEN_QUEUE 27, PR #1988)

**What was found.** The plan was to refresh `configs/ci_shard_costs.json` (measured on Nov 5) and add a Core-only table, because one test leg kept taking about 17 minutes against 12 for the other two. A local full-suite run on both installs, with junit, scored each deal on the measured costs: today's deal was already within about 1 % (1549 / 1552 / 1524 s), and a fresh table gave 1542 / 1541 / 1541, about 10 s. On CI the same deal gave 17.1 / 12.0 / 12.0 minutes on one run and 12.0 / 17.0 / 12.3 on the next, and every test in the slow leg was about 1.4x slower there (test_repair 152 s against 102 s). That is a slower runner, not the deal. Inside a leg, `-n 4` bought about 1.8x. And the run's last third was queueing: the legs finished at 09:10, and the required `test` aggregator then waited 12.7 minutes for a runner (Core-only's 7).

**What it cost.** Nothing shipped wrong; a measurement saved a PR that would have changed 800 numbers for about 10 s.

**The rule.** (1) Before re-dealing shards for speed, compare the slow leg's per-test sum (the "Slowest test modules" block) across two runs of the same deal: if the slow leg moves and its tests all slow down together, it is the runner. (2) Read every job's `created_at` against `started_at`: on a busy day the wall is runners waiting for a slot, and the lever there is fewer jobs per run, not faster tests. (3) A job folded into another keeps its check visible as its own named steps, in its own venv when its install differs, activated in every step that uses it (each step is a new shell). Keep the required check names as they are.
