## 2026-10-13 — a hold of the single-writer gate across a swap starts AFTER the step that needs the gate on another thread, and is worth only what the structural tests pin

**(1) The carry alone left a silent loss; the gate closes it.** `_carry_live_settings` read the live settings rows just before the replace, and a `kv_set_json` that committed between that read and the replace was lost: it returned success, the in-process cache served it, and it reverted at the next start. Settings writers take the write gate and nothing else (no corpus lease, no swap-window guard, a raw connection the library generation does not stamp), so **the only mechanism they all pass through is the gate, and the close is to hold it, not to guard each writer.** `_carry_and_swap` holds it on the restore thread from before the carry to the end of `swap_in_corpus` and empties the settings cache before letting go.

**(2) Where the hold starts is the whole review.** `_checkpoint_before_swap` runs `checkpoint_wal` on its own thread and that thread takes the gate; a hold taken before it would make the restore wait on a thread that waits on the restore. The hold starts after the checkpoint returned (its gate is released when `checkpoint_wal` returns). The other candidate deadlock, the generation window's exclusive wait for connections being opened, cannot meet the hold: the shared holders (`connecting()`, a creator and one read) never take the gate. **Both are pinned by the source order (the hold after the checkpoint, the carry and the swap only inside the helper) and by a test that runs the REAL swap under the hold on a thread with a deadline, so a step that takes the gate elsewhere and waits fails the test instead of hanging the suite.**

**(3) A bounded acquire, a refusal that costs nothing, and the gate-off case said out loud.** The wait is bounded (`OO_SWAP_GATE_S`, 60 s) and refuses with nothing written, like its two neighbours; waiting for ever would trade a lost setting for a hang. `OO_WRITE_GATE=0` makes `write_lock()` a no-op: the swap goes on and the report says `gate_held: false`, because refusing would take the diagnostic escape hatch away from the one operation that has to work.

**(4) Moving a call into a helper moves the anchors of every source-scanning guard.** Three older tests located the commit point by the literal `swap_in_corpus(` in `run_restore`'s source (the abort-point ordering, the barrier before the swap, the refusal before the swap). They were re-anchored onto the helper's call, each with a dated comment, and the stop test gained a check that the helper itself holds no abort point. **Run every structural source-scan test when a call changes place, not only the suites that touch the behaviour.**
