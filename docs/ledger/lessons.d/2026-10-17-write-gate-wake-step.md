## 2026-10-17 — A gate that frees first and wakes second needs a waiter that looks again: wait in steps, say what the step costs

**What was found.** ``WriterGate.release`` clears the owner and THEN wakes the head of the queue (the one writer FIFO lets take a free gate); the ``finally`` of an ``acquire`` that gave up wakes the next head the same way. A KeyboardInterrupt (a script's ``write_lock`` release on the main thread) or a MemoryError inside that wake-up leaves the gate free and the head asleep on its own condition, which nobody will notify. The wait had no timeout, so only another ``release`` could end it, and with the gate free there is none: every later writer queues behind the head until the process restarts. Shown by injection into BOTH paths (``tests/test_write_gate_lost_wakeup.py``, KeyboardInterrupt and MemoryError each): the REAL ``release`` whose wake-up raises once, and the wake-up of the ``finally`` of a head that was interrupted in its wait and gave up on a free gate; the real-signal frequency was NOT measured, so no rate is claimed. This is the same shape the library lock's close-window change closed (``_RECHECK_S`` in ``library_generation.py``), found there first and listed as open for the write gate.

**The rules.**
1. **When the free comes before the wake and the wake is a call, the waiter must look again on its own.** ``acquire`` now waits in steps of ``_WAKE_STEP_S`` (1.0 s) inside the same ``while True:`` loop that breaks when the gate is granted, so the shape the interrupt tests pin is unchanged. The bounded wait takes ``min(time left, step)``, so it still gives up at its own deadline and not at a multiple of the step.
2. **A step is not a timeout, and the number names what it protects.** The longest a lost wake-up may cost a waiting writer; an acquire without a timeout still waits as long as the gate is held (a test holds the gate for eight steps and requires the waiter to stay asleep, and requires ``_timeouts`` to stay 0). One second is chosen, not tuned, and the module says so: NOTHING MEASURED argues for 1.0 over 0.25 or 2. It equals the library lock's step (``_RECHECK_S``), so one number is explained once, and writers already wait seconds to minutes (the 10-16 diagnostics record holds of 917 s and 977 s, and a writer queued behind one waits as long), so a second is noticed but not material. A smaller or larger step trades the two figures of rule 3 one for one: 0.25 s adds at most a quarter of a second to a lost wake-up and costs four times as much CPU (about 0.7 % of a core at 49 waiters), 2 s adds up to two seconds and costs half (about 0.09 %); both are arithmetic from the measured per-wake cost, not extra measurements.
3. **Measure the cost of the step, do not assume it.** 49 writers queued behind a gate held 20 s: 0.0002 s of CPU before, 0.0366 s after, i.e. 980 wake-ups (49 per second) at about 37 µs each, 1.8 ms of CPU per second of hold, 0.18 % of one core at 49 waiters. The production holds of 917 s and 977 s (the 10-16 diagnostics) would cost about 1.7 and 1.8 s of CPU each by the same per-wake figure IF 49 writers were queued all through them (the queue length during those holds is not known; arithmetic from the measurement, not a second measurement). An independent re-measure at 49 waiters behind a 10 s hold gave 39 to 42 µs per wake-up.
4. **A control test makes the scenario prove itself.** With the waits made untimed again (``_wait`` stubbed to drop the timeout) the same lost wake-up leaves the writer asleep for ten steps and is ended only by the missing wake-up being sent by hand; the injection tests fail on the old waits with the constant defined (the writer never gets through; on the module literally as it was they fail earlier, at the ``setattr`` of the constant that does not exist), the finally path has its own control, and a pin requires both waits of ``acquire`` to pass a step.
5. **What the free-before-wake reorder costs, said once.** Because the gate is freed and woken before the hold is measured, a failure inside ``_wake_head`` skips the measuring of THAT release (the hold's seconds, its per-holder row and the long-hold record are missing for it). The gate itself is free and the waiter is recovered; the figures of one interrupted release are not.
