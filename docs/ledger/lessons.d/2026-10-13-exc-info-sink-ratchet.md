## 2026-10-13 — a handler-level scrub is a net, not the rule: remove the sink and ratchet it

`exc_info=True` hands the record a live exception whose text and traceback (with its `__cause__`/`__context__` chain) a handler formats unscrubbed; `SecretFilter` catches what the process holds, but not a passphrase it does not hold, a message cut across records, or a handler built before the journal. About 490 such sites existed in `src`. `tests/test_no_raw_exc_info.py` (scanner `tests/exc_info_scan.py`) fails on `exc_info=`, `Logger.exception`, `sys.exc_info()` and the `traceback` text makers; `tests/data/exc_info_allowlist.json` lists the files still carrying sites (count + owner) as a zero-slack ratchet: a new site fails, a stale count fails. Its self-test runs the scanner over every sink shape, so a shape dropped from the scanner fails a test. Convert a site to `secret_scrub.log_failure`, or to the class name alone where engine text enters a record.
