## 2026-10-27 — a restore or repair folds the log before the swap window, and the window then finds none unless something outside the corpus lease writes: 0 to 1,174 frames measured on two plaintext stores, the file replace (not the log) is the large store's cost, and a log read as the `-wal` file's size misleads

**What was found (coordinator item 1 of 20:42Z, ruled R341: a stated limit; replaces the "how many a real restore leaves there is NOT measured" sentence of `lessons.d/2026-10-27-the-swap-window-hold-has-no-time-bound.md`).** `swap_window`'s docstring said the restore and the repair fold the log into the file before the window, so the window should find only the frames written between the fold and the window, and that how many a real restore leaves there was NOT measured. Measured now, by real committed restores (`run_restore(commit=True)`) and real repairs (`repair.run` on a plan made by the real start-up check) of two stores; nothing in the repository was changed to measure it.

**Setup (named).** This session's box: 4 vCPU, 15 GiB RAM, virtual disk (`/dev/vda`), Linux 6.18, Python 3.13, SQLite 3.45.1, plaintext stores (`OO_DB_PLAINTEXT=1`, WAL), `OO_NO_SCHEDULER=1`; another review process of ours ran short test jobs on it during some cells (timings, not frame counts, may carry that noise). Mid store: 60,000 articles, 358,402 mentions, 30,000 keywords, 300 sources, FTS index, 214.2 MiB. Big store: 300,000 articles, 1,792,904 mentions, 1,055.4 MiB. Both settled (engine disposed, no log) before each cell, a fresh copy per cell, restored from a `write_backup_v2` of the same store. Writers, both ORM through `session_scope()` and so outside the corpus lease (the restore's quiescence wait only waits for registered jobs), started 1 s before the restore: `light` = one article per commit every 20 ms; `heavy` = 200 articles per commit, continuously. Warm = the store was copied or written just before; cold = `sync` + `posix_fadvise(DONTNEED)` on the live file (the artifact, staged copy and working copy stayed warm). **The log was read as frames, `mxFrame - nBackfill` from the wal-index (`-shm`) header at offsets 0 and 96, read as a file** (page 4,096 B, a frame 4,120 B), not as the `-wal` file's size: the file is a high-water mark (it restarts from its start after an automatic checkpoint without shrinking), and a byte reading showed a heavy writer's log as 757 frames once and 0 the next time.

**Restore, frames at window entry** (n = 1 to 4 a cell, 21 restores with a writer; the ranges are the observed minimum and maximum, not a distribution):

| store | writer | frames at entry | fold to window | first `dispose_engine()` | window held |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 214 MiB | none | 0 (3 runs) | 0.29 to 0.36 s | 0.001 s | 0.013 s |
| 214 MiB | light, warm | 725 to 799 (5 runs) | 0.35 to 0.41 s | 0.015 to 0.026 s | 0.12 to 0.14 s |
| 214 MiB | light, cold | 96 and 379 | 0.34 and 0.39 s | 0.011 and 0.414 s | 0.130 and 0.713 s |
| 214 MiB | heavy | 0 (7 of 8 runs), 757 (1) | 0.31 to 0.59 s | 0.001 to 0.003 s | 0.002 to 0.434 s |
| 1,055 MiB | none | 0 (3 runs) | 0.31 to 0.32 s | 0.002 to 0.003 s | 0.40 to 0.60 s |
| 1,055 MiB | light | 940 and 311 (warm), 319 (cold) | 0.30 to 0.36 s | 0.003 to 0.019 s | 0.47 to 0.55 s |

With nothing else writing the corpus the window found **no log in any run** (restore: three runs on each store, plus a smoke run on a 2,000-article store; repair: below). With a writer outside the lease it found **0 to 1,174 frames (4.6 MiB of frames) in 21 restores**, which is that writer's rate times the 0.25 to 0.59 s between the fold and the window (`hold_writes`, `_carry_live_settings` into the working copy, the `swap_in_corpus` entry; its parts were not timed separately). That gap did not grow with the store (0.29 to 0.36 s with no writer on both) and did not depend on the writer. The frames are folded by the window's first `dispose_engine()` in 0.001 to 0.03 s warm and 0.41 s once cold (379 frames), the per-MiB figure the docstring already gave (6 to 10 ms a MiB warm) for a log of a few MiB. In the two warm heavy runs on the big store the hold was 1 to 2 ms because the writer's open connection kept the old file alive: the close of a connection that is not the last does not fold the log, and the old file is replaced with its log unfolded, discarded with it. **At entry is not the peak:** in one cold light run the 5 ms sampler saw 1,655 frames (6.5 MiB of file) in the gap and 96 at entry (a writer's automatic checkpoint, every 1,000 frames, in between; inferred), and the `-wal` file read 6.5 MiB at entry because it keeps its high-water size. Before the restore's own fold the log held 0 to 10,327 frames (40.6 MiB of frames, in a 64 MiB file) with a writer; the fold took 0.002 to 0.76 s.

**The repair, a damaged copy of the mid store (two leaf pages of `articles` overwritten, the start-up check confirmed it), three runs a cell, the first run's file lost and two kept:** no writer: repaired, 0 frames before the fold, after it and at the window, fold 0.001 s, gap 0.25 to 0.28 s, window held 0.004 s. A gated ORM writer (it queues behind the gate the repair holds): repaired, 219 frames (a 4.0 MiB file) before the repair's own fold, 0 after and at the window, fold 0.007 to 0.009 s; the writer's pending flush then got `StaleLibraryError` once. An ungated raw writer (a Core commit every 25 ms): **Declined, "something was saved to the library file after the repaired copy was started", in all runs**, nothing switched, 405 to 422 frames before the fold, 0 after, 1 to 2 at the window. So the repair's window finds an empty log or the repair declines; nothing measured is a repair window that starts with a log to fold.

**The large store's other cost is the file replace, not the log.** `_replace_live_corpus`: 0.012 s at 214 MiB, 0.39 s (warm) and 0.46 s (cold) at 1,055 MiB, with no log; the hold at 1,055 MiB was 0.40 to 0.60 s with no log, with the light writer's log and in the one cold heavy run (0.547 s); at 214 MiB it was 0.013 s with no log and 0.12 to 0.14 s with 725 frames. Likely cost: the kernel dropping the replaced file's cached pages; not verified, not isolated.

**What it does not show.** Encrypted stores (where `close()` holds the GIL for the checkpoint), a store bigger than 1,055 MiB, a slower disk, a writer heavier than the two used, and which shipped writers actually commit during a restore without being a registered job (interactive saves, the briefing cache, ring producers: not enumerated). Nothing is extrapolated. The raw outputs are the WAL lane's `swap-window-log-at-entry-data/`, outside the repository.

**A writer outside the lease loses what it commits after the restore's working copy was taken** (known design, the R174 family; the comment is in `backup.merge`: a write made after the snapshot is thrown away by the swap). Measured size: the light writer committed 244 to 279 rows in a 5.7 to 15 s restore and 88 to 97 survived; the heavy writer 14,000 to 24,600 rows and 800 to 6,800 survived. The log at window entry is part of what is discarded, not carried. Registered jobs are waited out; an unregistered writer is not. No new proposal: this is stated, not new.

**The stale writer list.** The module docstring of `database/library_generation.py` listed `analytics/spell_index.py` and `database/fts.py` among the writers no gate holds. The read of PR 1594 found them gated (`spell_index`: `text()` statements in a `session_scope`; the `fts` rebuilds: under `write_lock()`), as are the settings saves (`config/kv_store.py`: `write_lock()`, then a raw `connect()` that never passes `connecting()`). The ungated ones are the DDL steps of `init_db`: `database/maintenance.py` `ensure_*`, `database/migrate.py`, `database/fts.py` `ensure_fts` and `database/source_identity.py` (`BEGIN IMMEDIATE` on a Core connection). The docstring now says so.

**The rules.**

1. **Read a WAL's backlog as frames from the wal-index header, never as the `-wal` file's size, and give bytes in the unit you computed.** The file is a high-water mark and says nothing about what is still to be folded; a byte figure goes beside the frame count or not at all. (This entry's first draft wrote 4.8 MiB for 4,836,912 bytes, which is 4.6 MiB; the figure was checked against the raw records before it went into a docstring.)
2. **A "not measured" sentence is closed by a measurement that names its store, its size, its box, its engine and its temperature, and keeps what is still not measured as a sentence.** The docstring now names two stores, one box and plaintext SQLite 3.45.1, and says encrypted stores, a bigger store and a heavier writer are not measured; do not read it as covering them.
3. **Measure the whole of a body before arguing about its biggest term.** The question was how much log the window finds; the answer was almost none, and the measurement found that at 1 GiB the replace dominates the hold, which a log-only cell would have missed.
4. **A cell with a concurrent writer needs the writer's phase recorded, and "at entry" is not "the peak".** The heavy writer's 200-row commit takes about 0.4 s and writes its frames at commit, so the log at entry was 0 or 757 to 1,174 frames depending on where in its cycle the restore found it; a single run per cell would have reported either.
