## 2026-11-06 — a rewrite that is 26 % faster on a one-country bench can scan every country's rows: price a query on the lane's shape, not the bench's (OSM R440)

**What was found.** The geography view's `changed` measure joined `osm_tag_changes` to a country's places (4.6 s warm at 2.0M places and 1.8M change rows). Rewriting it as "distinct (osm_type, osm_id) from the change rows first, then EXISTS on the place" measured 3.4 s, 26 % better, and the figure was identical (83,539). But the change table has no index on the country, so that form reads EVERY country's change rows to find one country's, and the bench lane holds exactly one country, where the table IS the country. On a lane of fifty countries the same statement would have cost fifty times as much per build. The form that is driven from the country's own places and probes the change rows by object (`ix_osm_tag_changes_object`) measured the same as the original join (4.6 s) and its cost follows the country. The real saving came from storing the count in the places summary (read: 7 ms), not from the rewrite.

**The rule.** When a bench has one of something the real data has many of, a statement whose cost depends on a table shared between them cannot be priced on it: read the plan for what the statement scans without the country (`EXPLAIN QUERY PLAN`), not only how long it took. A stored count rides an existing cache key only after its writers are enumerated and pinned: `tests/test_osm_places_summary.py::test_only_the_daily_apply_writes_the_change_rows` fails on a second writer of `osm_tag_changes`, and a builder of unsaved rows (`changes.change_rows`) is followed to its imports so it cannot become one unseen.
