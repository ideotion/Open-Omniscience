## 2026-10-27 — `count(*)` on an external-content FTS5 table scans the content table, and a recorded figure is a claim about one statement

**What was found.** The cold encrypted measurement of `fts_status` at 1,000,000 articles (the app's own SQLCipher engine, 5 cold runs) put 99% of the call in a statement nobody had changed since D4: `SELECT count(*) FROM article_fts`, 9.6 to 11.9 s cold and 5.5 to 6.0 s warm, 3 to 5 s from its own 15 s deadline. The statement the work had just added (the index's ends) cost 76 to 100 ms. For an external-content table FTS5 answers a `count(*)` by reading every row of the content table, columns included, so it costs what `SELECT count(*) FROM articles NOT INDEXED` costs (9.5 to 10.5 s cold) and follows the article TEXT, while `SELECT count(*) FROM articles` is a scan of the smallest covering index (`idx_article_created_at`), 0.25 s cold, and follows the NUMBER of articles. The two give the same number by construction, so the swap changed no value and `fts_status` went from 9.7 to 12.0 s to 0.29 to 0.56 s cold (median 11.4 s to 0.33 s). Separately, the docstrings, two test comments, a PR body and a ledger entry had all said that a count of articles "is the 74 s cold scan the boot refuses": a figure with no statement recorded beside it, repeated for weeks as the reason for a design. One measurement at the real size on the current schema did not reproduce it (what it timed is unknowable: a table scan on a slower disk, or this very `count(*) FROM article_fts`).

**The rules.**

1. **Time a diagnostic whole, statement by statement, on a store of the real size before saying what its cost is, and before saying which part is yours.** The new statement was 0.1 s; the cost sat in a line older than the work. A timing that covers only what you added cannot find that.
2. **To count the documents of an external-content FTS5 table, ask the content table; to count the index, ask its `_docsize` shadow table.** `count(*)` on the virtual table is a content-table scan (it reads the text), so it is neither cheap nor a measure of the index. A cheap count needs a covering index, and that is the planner's choice: pin it by capturing the statement the code actually issued at the cursor and running `EXPLAIN QUERY PLAN` on that text (a column in the select, a `WHERE`, or `NOT INDEXED` turns it red), not by a comment.
3. **A recorded figure belongs to a statement, a schema, a store size, a disk and a temperature.** "74 s cold" had none of them, so it could be neither reproduced nor retired, and it was cited as the reason a design was refused. When a figure is the reason for a refusal, record those five things with it; when a measurement withdraws the premise, drop the premise in the same change (grep the docstrings, the tests' `reason=` strings and the ledger pointers), and say that the old figure was not reproduced rather than silently replacing it. The refusal itself (here: the boot compares no counts) is a decision and stays one until it is ruled on; losing its premise does not reverse it.
4. **A test that pins a decision as an absence is rewritten, not deleted, when the decision changes.** "No statement counts the articles" became "exactly one does, it is the plain count, its plan is a covering index, and nothing counts the table it replaced". The old absence test would have gone red on the first run; the rewritten one also keeps the next person from adding a second count.
5. **When a statement behind a reported number is swapped, run the old and the new on the shapes where they could differ and compare them to what the function reports.** Full, shrunken after deletes with a reused id, emptied, partial; assert the figure is non-zero so that equality is not a tautology. The verdict that must not depend on the number (here `partial (inferred)`) gets its own test with the number answered and with it timed out.
6. **A cold figure is an evicted file AND a fresh process, five times, with the disk class named.** Here a cold run was 11,382 ms and the same call warm was 5,801 ms before, and 327 ms against 2.3 ms after: the warm figure hides the codec and the disk, and "the disk is a fast virtual one (1.3 GB/s), the codec dominates" is a limit to state, because a laptop's cold figure for a table scan is higher and a covering-index count's is higher too.
