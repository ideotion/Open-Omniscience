## 2026-11-05 — measure a read surface at the size it will meet before building on it: one correlated NOT EXISTS was 95% of a 333 s page (OSM B3b/B3c)

**What was found.** The B3a/B3b "prior" readers (adoption, freshness, trends) drop the key rows of a create / delete / restore version with a correlated `NOT EXISTS` against the same `osm_history_changes` table. On a synthetic Germany-scale lane (2.0M places, 1.8M tag-change rows, 12M history rows, 8 % object-level) that single statement took 317 s of a 333 s surface; at 1.5M history rows it was 10.5 s of 14.4 s, so nothing in the tests, which use a few hundred rows, hinted at it. The cost is not the statement's shape but its being re-run against the whole planet's history for every country read.

**The rule.** When a read joins a table to itself to exclude a minority of rows, copy the minority (here: the DISTINCT object-level event versions of one country) into an indexed TEMP table once per read and anti-join to that, then drop it in `finally`. Compare with `IS NOT DISTINCT FROM` where the version or timestamp may be NULL (a plain `=` silently keeps the very rows the exclusion exists to drop). Measured: 317 s → 45 s for the statement, 333 s → 67 s for the surface at 12M rows. It is still about a minute at that size and grows with the history table, so the durable fix is a per-country monthly summary written when the history cut finishes; that is a design change, proposed and not built.

**Where enforced.** `tests/test_osm_trends.py` (rows with no version numbers told apart by timestamp, the deleted version's own key not counted, and that the temp table outlives neither the read nor reaches the lane file). The bench that produced the numbers: `/mnt/project-files/osm-lane/synthetic-lane-bench/` (`build.py`, `run.py`, `run2.py`, `RESULTS.md`). Statements that scan `osm_tag_changes` by an unindexed `country_alpha3` (2 to 4 s at 1.8M rows) and the multi-pass snapshot reads (0.5 to 1.6 s per statement at 2M places) were measured and recorded as acceptable, not fixed.
