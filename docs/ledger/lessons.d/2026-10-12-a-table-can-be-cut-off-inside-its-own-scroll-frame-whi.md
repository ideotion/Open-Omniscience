## 2026-10-12 — A TABLE CAN BE CUT OFF INSIDE ITS OWN SCROLL FRAME WHILE THE PAGE SHOWS NO HORIZONTAL SCROLL (row F analytics 4-5 look, PR #1340, `src/static/app.css`, `scripts/analytics45_look.py`)

The two cross-edition tables sit in `.wiki-table-wrap { overflow-x: auto }`. At 390 px they were 340 and 364 px of content in a 316 px frame, so the last column (the figure the table is for) was clipped behind a scroll with no mark that it scrolled, and the check every earlier walk ran, `document.documentElement.scrollWidth <= innerWidth`, passed, because the overflow belongs to the wrap and never reaches the page. **Measure the frame, not the page:** `wrap.scrollWidth <= wrap.clientWidth` for every `overflow-x: auto` wrapper a surface draws, at the narrowest width. The look reads both numbers into its record (24 and 48 px over before the fix, 0 after, in both English and Arabic), and its other two judges were mutation-checked (the `unknown` change count drawn as `0` fails it, the edition drop-down's reload removed fails it). A second small fact from the same run: the generic drop-down judge (`csp_sweep.sweep_selects`) read a pick that DID change the table as `dom`/`none` in 2 of 48 runs, because its pre-pick idle sample caught a background request; a drop-down whose effect matters wants an explicit check of the thing it must change, in the same run, beside the generic one.
