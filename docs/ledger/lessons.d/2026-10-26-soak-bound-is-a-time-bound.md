## 2026-10-26 — A byte ceiling on a log that a writer keeps filling is a time bound: name its two terms, count only what the scan could see, and say "inconclusive" when the round could not have failed

**What was found.** ``tests/test_wal_starvation_soak.py`` failed once on CI (round 2 of 3: 9,261,792 B of WAL at the end of the scan against a ceiling of 6 x 1,048,576 = 6,291,456 B) on a docs-only change, with the code under it unchanged since the 18 Oct base. The property the test is about held in every round as the test counted it (10 in-scan releases each round and a success each round), and only the byte ceiling failed. The writer thread appends a 256 KiB row every ~20 ms (about 12.5 MB/s, no fsync per commit), and the size read at the end of the scan is, in the main, the bytes written since the last ``wal_checkpoint(TRUNCATE)`` finished (the connections' autocheckpoint, ``journal_size_limit`` and the in-scan release reset the log too: with ``checkpoint_wal`` stubbed out entirely the log stayed at 1.9 MB). A TRUNCATE costs three to four fsyncs. So the "6 MiB" bound was never a size: it held only while a truncating checkpoint finished within about half a second of the end of the scan, which is to say while fsync was fast. With 4 to 13 % margin on a fast disk, one slow stretch on a shared runner turns latency into bytes.

**Reproduction (this container, strace-injected, no disk touched).** Re-running the test and loading the CPUs did not reproduce it (peak WAL 0 to 796 KiB idle, 0 to 535 KiB with 4 CPU burners, 0 to 796 KiB with 8). Delaying every fsync/fdatasync did: 100 ms gave 1.6 to 2.9 MiB with 3 to 4 attempts a round; 300 ms gave 6,394,272 / 6,666,192 / 6,666,192 B with 1 attempt a round, over the old ceiling in every round. What was NOT shown: the runner's real fsync latency (nothing logs it), so "a slow stretch" is the mechanism that reproduces the signature, not a measured fact about that run.

**What the independent read of the fix found, and the rules it gives.**
1. **Reproduce a CI-only failure with the mechanism, not by running it again.** Idle repeats (6 iterations of 3 rounds) and CPU-load probes found nothing; a one-line strace injection (``strace -f --seccomp-bpf -e trace=fsync,fdatasync -e inject=fsync,fdatasync:delay_enter=300ms``) reproduced the signature (few attempts, the log growing round after round, releases and the success unchanged) on the first run. When a test fails only on a shared runner, ask which resource the test silently assumes is fast and slow that one down on the real test file.
2. **A bound that mixes bytes and time has to say both terms, and the time term counts only time the scan could see.** The round's allowance is 6 x the checkpoint ceiling (the headroom the design promises) PLUS the writer's measured rate x the longest checkpoint attempt of that round, capped at the scan's end (the bytes that arrive while the one attempt that is in flight cannot finish; the part of an attempt after the scan ended is not time the log was being measured). Each term is a named number in the test (``_allowed_wal_bytes`` returns ``headroom``, ``in_flight`` and ``total``) and a failure prints all three. A fast disk keeps almost the old bound; a slow one gets the extra time it took inside the scan.
3. **Do not claim a bound kills a mutation until the mutation has been run through it.** The first version said the new bound "still fails a log nothing truncated" on arithmetic (a 12.5 MB/s writer for 0.9 s, about 11 MB against about 7.0 MB). The real build from before the fix does not behave so: its scan pins the snapshot, every busy TRUNCATE holds the write lock for its 50 ms busy timeout, the writer slows to 5 to 6.7 MB/s, and the log ends at 4.5 to 6.4 MB, INSIDE the new bound in 10 of 10 rounds (and inside the old fixed one in 9 of 10). The byte bound never killed that mutation, old or new. What kills it, in every round, is the in-scan success. The mutation is now run as a real round (``_WalGuardResult.fetchmany`` patched to never close the cursor, and to commit without closing it, the original design bug) and the round must be red for exactly that reason.
4. **Count evidence only if it could have been seen by the thing under test.** The success assertion counted every attempt record, including the attempt still in flight when the scan ended, joined and recorded after the pin was gone. In the slow regime this change tunes for, the original design bug (commit without closing the cursor) passed all four assertions on a success that finished 0.475 s after the scan ended. The test now counts a success only if its checkpoint call was issued after the first chunk was delivered (the reader already pinned) and finished before the scan ended, and an attempt that returned nothing answers nothing.
5. **A check that cannot fail is not a pass: say "inconclusive", with the numbers, and do not count it.** Two kinds. The byte check: once the allowance reaches every byte the round could have written (a slow-attempt round: the in-flight term alone is most of the scan), it cannot fail, and the round is printed as inconclusive for the bytes. The property check: with fewer than four checkpoint attempts answering inside the scan and none clear, the disk is too slow to judge (measured with every fsync delayed: the fixed build clears one inside the scan in every round up to 60 ms, in 3 of 4 at 80 ms and in none at 100 ms, where an attempt of 0.43 s cannot fit a release window of 0.1 s), so the round is inconclusive for the property, printed, and when every round is the test is skipped with the numbers instead of failing a slow runner or passing it. A round that is red is still red: the mutants are red whenever four or more attempts answered, and the original design bug on slow attempts is inconclusive, no longer a pass.
6. **A test that sets a process-wide switch uses the fixture that undoes it.** ``_wal_engine`` set ``OO_WAL_SIZE_LIMIT_MB`` straight in ``os.environ``, so the 1 MiB limit leaked into every later test of the process; it is ``monkeypatch.setenv``.
7. **A test that fails only late in a long run is a fixed number pretending to be a property.** The run is 75 minutes in one process, the failure was one round of three, and the test file had no diff: the signal for "this assertion is measuring the machine" is that the failure moves with load and not with the code. Fold the fix into the next change of that lane rather than a PR of its own, but do not retry the job: show the cause.
