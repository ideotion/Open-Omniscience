## 2026-11-05 — a sharded run's time includes the wait for runners: read created-to-started beside every leg's time (C10 follow-up, R433, PR #1733)

**What was found.** The three-shard layout of PR #1726 was judged on each leg's time, start to end, and the required `test` check was green 28:31 and 29:51 after the run was created on the first two runs. On the third run (the head that merged) it was green 40:04 after, with the slowest leg at 30:37: `Test (shard 1)` had waited 10:00 between its creation and its start and shard 2 had waited 6:38. The step times of all six legs were read; the tests took what the cost table predicted to within 3 % (1604 / 1666 / 1730 s against 1600 / 1680 / 1680), so the 10 minutes were not the suite. Both late starts came 2 to 3 seconds after a sibling job of the same run ended (SQLCipher newest at 14:21:49, shard 2 at 14:21:51; SQLCipher floor at 14:25:09, shard 1 at 14:25:12), and two other PR runs were running at the time.

**The rule.** When a CI change is judged by wall time, read each job's created-to-started time as well as its started-to-ended time, from the job record, and say which one a figure is. A leg's time is not the run's time. A layout that splits a job into legs asks for more concurrent jobs at once (every PR run now asks for four more than before the split), and a cap on concurrent jobs turns that into queue that no step time shows.

**What was and was not established.** Measured: the waits, the start times against the sibling jobs' end times, the other runs in flight. INFERRED: a cap on concurrent jobs, because the limit of the plan is not visible from the repository. Not measured: how often the wait happens (one run of three showed it; the earlier two runs' queue times were not read). Decided (R433): created-to-started of every leg is recorded on the next five runs before a fourth shard is considered; four shards were about 22 to 23 minutes per leg on paper against a 10-minute wait that two more legs could make longer.

**Stated limits.** Nothing mechanical guards this: it is a way of reading, kept in `docs/maintenance/CI_SHARDS.md` ("A leg's time is not the run's time") beside the numbers. The macOS observation lane held a runner for 50:30 in the same run and gates nothing; whether it counts against the cap is not known.
