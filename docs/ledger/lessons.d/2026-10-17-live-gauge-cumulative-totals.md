## 2026-10-17 — A DURATION STATISTIC RECORDED WHEN THE EVENT ENDS CANNOT SEE THE EVENT THAT NEVER ENDS: KEEP CUMULATIVE TOTALS, READ BY DIFFERENCE, READ THE CLOCK UNDER THE LOCK THAT ORDERS THEM, CLOSE EVERY OPEN INTERVAL IDEMPOTENTLY, AND CHECK THE GAUGE AGAINST AN INDEPENDENT LOG

**What was found.** `lock_stats` recorded a hold in its pass window when the hold ENDED, and the pass monitor resets the window at the start of every pass. So the one case the extraction lock's diagnostics exist for, a hold that does not end (a hang, an 85 s page, a pass that crashes), leaves nothing in the window; every other extraction waits behind it and the record shows a quiet lock. A mean and a maximum taken at the end of events are blind to exactly the event still in progress.

**The shape that works (Item C, `src/ingest/lock_stats.py`).**
1. **Keep CUMULATIVE totals that only grow (`occupied_s`, `waited_s`) and give the reader a reading that adds the part still open (`live_reading()`).** The difference of two readings is then exact for the interval between them, whatever happened inside it, resets of the pass window included (`reset()` deliberately leaves the gauge alone, test-pinned). Ship the totals and let the reader divide; a rate stored in the gauge would be right for one interval only, the one it was computed over.
2. **Keep the open waiters in a DICT keyed by thread, not as a running sum of start times.** `(a + b) - b != a` in floating point, so a sum drifts; a dict is subtracted from `now` at reading time and drifts by nothing.
3. **Read the clock UNDER the gauge's own lock, in the mark and in the reading.** If a mark read the clock outside the lock, a reading could get a time earlier than a mark already applied and report more held time than elapsed. One private leaf lock (never held while taking another) totally orders marks and readings.
4. **Every open interval needs a closer that does not depend on the mark that normally closes it, and closing must be idempotent.** The first head closed a wait only in `holding()` (acquire succeeded) or in `gave_up()` (called only when the lock was never taken). An interrupt between `asking()` and the lock, or a `holding()` that never ran, left a waiter in the dict: `live_reading()` then counted it as waiting for ever, and the thread's next `asking()` overwrote the entry, so `waited_s` went BACKWARDS, which breaks the one promise of the totals. Now `record()` calls `gave_up()` unconditionally (a thread that is not waiting is left alone), `asking()` closes a stale wait before opening a new one, and `holding()` from a thread other than the recorded holder closes the phantom hold at that instant (the real lock cannot be held by two threads), each keeping the totals continuous. Found by an independent read of the first head, not by its own tests: the tests exercised the paths the author had thought of.
5. **Write the mark's body so an interrupt cannot land inside it, compute before you store, then say what it still can do.** CPython 3.13 checks for a pending interrupt at a call's return, at the start of a Python frame AND at a backward jump (a `for` or `while`, a comprehension, and `with`, which calls), not between call-free, loop-free statements; the step bodies contain none of those (`in`, subscripts, `del`, comparisons; no `max`, `.pop`, `.get`), and each computes every new total BEFORE it stores any, so a mark is applied whole or not at all AND a step that fails (a `MemoryError` while a float is allocated) changes nothing. The first fold pinned the first half with an AST search for calls, which a loop would have passed; the pin is now the BYTECODE (`dis`: no CALL, no JUMP_BACKWARD, no FOR_ITER, no BEFORE_WITH, no MAKE_FUNCTION in a step), with a positive control that the finder finds each in a function that has it, and each step has a test that arms an arithmetic failure and asserts the state is unchanged. The marks sit where the exception table needs them (`asking()` first in the outer `try`, `holding()` first in the inner `try`, `released()` first in the `finally`; a mark outside its `try` is a window). An interrupt at the mark's OWN entry can still skip it: probed with a signal every 1 to 40 microseconds against a stub body, 190,638 interrupts left 0 leaked waiters and 0 holds set at depth 0, and 11,929 (6.3 percent) phantom holders; the reviewer's run of 144,022 gave 7.6 percent. Both are inflated by construction (a real `KeyboardInterrupt` is one event). A phantom holder is closed by the next hold from ANOTHER thread; the same thread's next hold DEEPENS it instead (its age keeps growing while the lock is free), and a thread that reuses a dead holder's `get_ident()` value is not "another thread", so those last until restart. The claim in the code is exactly that, not "exactly inside `released()`".
6. **A mark that sits inside the hold lengthens the hold by its own cost: measure it, apportion nothing, and say which commit each figure is for.** The first head said the marks cost "about 1 microsecond inside the hold", which was the total divided by the marks; the pair inside the hold (`holding()` + `released()`) is now timed on its own after a warm-up (the first timed block read 20 to 80 percent high): 1.1 to 1.7 microseconds on the first machine, 1.21 to 1.22 on the second, and 1.34 to 1.36 on the final head (whose steps compute before they store: +0.13 microseconds, about 11 percent); all four marks 2.1 to 3.0, and 2.52 on the final head, against a 4.3 to 4.8 ms extraction of an 8 KB page. The warm micro figure is a floor for the cost in a loaded run: there the gap between the window's `got` stamp and the gauge's start of a hold was 2 to 6 microseconds per hold, which is the cost of the part of `holding()` BEFORE its own clock read. A pass-level run (14 collectors of the real collector, base tree against gauge tree, five alternating runs per cell, paired by run number because throughput drifted down across the runs on both trees) is the second level: the micro number says what the marks cost, the pass number says whether anything notices; neither stands in for the other, and the pass number cannot resolve a microsecond against a 35 to 320 ms hold and is not claimed to. That pass-level run was made on the head before the last fold and not repeated; the row says so and gives the micro figure for the change, instead of letting the A/B stand for a tree it did not run.
7. **Test the gauge against an independent log, and make every test earn its keep against mutants.** `tests/test_lock_live_reading.py` hammers the real `extract_article` path from eight threads with a fake clock and builds the expected totals from a tape of the gauge's own clock reads, one LABELLED entry per mark (`_on_ask`, `_on_hold`, `_on_release`, `_on_give_up`) keyed by thread name; the first version assumed six POSITIONAL reads per extraction, which broke the moment a mark was added and tied the check to the order of the marks rather than to what each one did. 43 mutants (clock outside the lock, open hold dropped, wait not closed at the hold, release not in a `finally`, marks swapped, a `max` call, a loop, a `with` or a comprehension in a step body, each step storing before its arithmetic, a `BaseException` catch, a `record()` that gives up only after its window update, and the reviews' survivors among them) are all killed; the survivors of the first read were a `record()` that gave up only when the lock was never taken, a `_mark` that caught `BaseException`, and `asking()` before the outer `try`, each a place where the test checked a result and not WHERE the mark sat, and the second read found a pin that looked for calls only and a step that stored before it computed. The harness cross-check on real runs agrees too: the gauge's growth over the measured passes is 1.7 to 4.4 ms below the pass windows' hold totals over totals of 26 to 251 s, and within 2.2 ms of their wait totals over 69 to 709 s (the windows' totals are rounded to 1 ms); the hold side runs low by the 2 to 6 microseconds per hold between the window's `got` stamp and the holding mark's own clock read (inferred from the size, not separately measured), and both are floors of the lock's real occupancy. State the assumptions as well as the edges: the gauge assumes an EXCLUSIVE lock and means nothing with the no-op lock of the harness's lock-off cells.

**What the A/B did not show, and why it is written down.** In the first A/B (the pre-fold gauge, three runs, an older base) the longest single hold and the longest single wait read higher with the gauge at BOTH sizes, and the first draft of the row called that absent at 64 KB, which the 64 KB runs contradicted. In the second A/B on the final head (five runs per cell, base = the main it merges onto) the longest hold read lower with the gauge and the longest wait about equal, paired medians 0.75 and 0.95 for the hold, 1.05 and 1.01 for the wait. Each is one event per run, so the direction is not reproduced and the first reading is INFERRED to be scheduler noise on shared vCPUs, not shown; a small effect on a single event is not excluded. When a number moves in the wrong direction in one round and not in the next, say both, say it is one event, and do not write "absent" for what was measured as present.
