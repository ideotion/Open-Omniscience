## 2026-10-13 — the log half of a sink guard does not see the record half

The exc_info ratchet (`tests/test_no_raw_exc_info.py`) finds the log calls that ask for a traceback. Across `src/bulletin` (every module of it), `src/api/bulletin.py` and `src/briefing/card_audit.py` the same `except` blocks also wrote the exception's own words into the RECORD they return (`str(exc)`, `f"{type(exc).__name__}: {exc}"`, a route's `detail=str(exc)`), beside 34 log calls that asked for the traceback, and those records ride the diagnostics bundle, the backup and the annexes. The ratchet cannot see them, so converting only the log call would have left the passphrase's other door open in the very block just fixed. `_leaks` in `tests/test_runs_log_sinks.py` reads the other half: a handler's bound exception may only be handed to a scrubbing call or to `type()`. Rule: when a handler is converted, convert what it RETURNS in the same edit (the record keeps the class; the words go to the scrubbed log line).

Three things the conversion taught, each a way the next one goes wrong:

1. **Read the directory, do not list the files.** `_SWEEP_MODULES` is `src/api/bulletin.py`, the card audit and a glob of `src/bulletin/*.py`, so a module added later is held to the rule from its first commit. A hand-kept list of three modules left thirteen others outside the guard for as long as nobody remembered them.
2. **Class-only moves the words, it must not delete them.** A handler that wrote the words into its record and logged nothing (`gate._degraded`, the narration job's "no local model", the evidence plan's folder check) would lose them entirely once the record is class-only. Each such handler got a `log_failure` line, or, where the only reader is the operator who just asked (a route's refusal, "this folder cannot be written"), `exception_text`: the words stay, the held passphrase is out, and no chained cause is raised (`from None`, because `from exc` makes the traceback print the cause's message).
3. **Messages other code matches on.** `narration_job._OUTAGE_MARKERS` finds an outage by a substring of `fallback_reason` ("the model call failed"); the class-only reasons keep that phrase and put the class in brackets after it, and a test reads `_is_outage` on the new text.
