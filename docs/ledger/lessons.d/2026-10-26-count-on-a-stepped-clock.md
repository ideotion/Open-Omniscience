## 2026-10-26 — a test that sleeps a fixed time and counts what a thread did in it fails on a loaded runner; run the loop on a clock that only moves when the loop waits

`tests/test_holder_state.py::test_a_watchdog_tick_that_raises_waits_its_interval_and_does_not_stop_the_looks` ran the write-gate watchdog's real loop on a real thread, slept 1.7 s, stopped it, and asserted counts (`2 <= ticks <= 6`, then at least three looks between two raising ticks). On a pull request's CI runner (a whole-suite `test` job of 56 minutes 49 seconds) it failed with `assert 1 >= 3` and passed locally. Two things reproduce that exact text in a scratch copy: a 0.68 s stall of the thread inside the first tick (the second tick then lands near the end of the window, with one look after it), and a first tick that fails to open the journal entry the looks need (no looks, one per wake). The test could not tell the two apart, and neither is a defect of the loop.

**The rule.** A loop whose only blocking call is `stop.wait(timeout)` can be driven on the calling thread with a `stop` that advances a fake clock by `timeout` and says "stopped" when the next wait would cross the run length (`_SteppedClock` in that test file; the module's own `time.monotonic()` is pointed at the same clock for the length of the test, so `held_for`, `due` and the holder-state looks agree). The counts are then the loop's own: three ticks at 0.5, 1.0 and 1.5 s, 67 looks in 1.8 s, no stall to lose a window to. Add a cap on the number of waits, because a loop that spins waits for zero seconds and never moves the clock: the cap turns the old bug into a failed assertion by name (4,975 ticks in 1.8 s) and not a hung test. Both mutants were run: the old `due` placement fails the raising-tick test, and a look in the tick's `try` fails it and the pace test. The three tests that shared a fixed window slept 5.2 s between them; they now sleep none. Elapsed-time bounds that can only be too small under load (a gap of at least 0.4 s) are safe on a real clock; a count in a window is not.
