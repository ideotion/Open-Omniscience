## 2026-10-26 — a cache keyed on "anything was written" misses on every poll of a store that is written all the time: serve the totals, run the whole-index scans once in the background, and carry them in proportion to their cost (audit 0.5.0, A-002)

**What was found.** `/api/insights/status` (polled every 6 s by the Insights tab) was keyed on `PRAGMA data_version`, which changes on every commit by any connection. A collection pass commits once per batch (`index_article` inside it), so during a pass every poll was a miss and ran two whole-index scans on the request thread: `count(distinct article_id)` and `count(id)` over `keyword_mentions_all`, with no deadline and no concurrency cap. `tests/test_status_counters.py` pinned the invalidation on purpose ("a commit on another connection must bump the key so progress stays live"), so the design met its own test while defeating its purpose. The field's `count(*)` over about 12 M mentions measured 55 s, 182 s under other load (diagnostics of 2026-10-16); one open tab during a pass was a continuous loop of such scans, each holding a read snapshot that pins the WAL.

**What was built.** The route goes through `served_cache.cached(..., cold_payload=...)` like `/api/database/figures`: the three index-backed totals (articles, keywords, entities) answer at once; `indexed_articles`, `remaining` and `mentions` are `null` with `index_counts_state` (`computing`, `counted`, `failed`) until a background pass lands; each scan runs under its own `statement_deadline`; a finished pair is carried for ten times its measured duration (`_STATUS_SCAN_DUTY_FACTOR`, the factor #1439 gave the figures count), stamped `index_counts_computed_at` with `index_counts_recount_after_s`; a pass that cannot finish is recorded by kind, deadline and time and held for a stated window (`__ttl_s__`). `queries.status` stays the blocking, complete computation, now composed of `status_totals`, `status_indexed_articles` and `status_mentions`.

**Numbers, and which are measured.** The field figure the deadline rests on is `SELECT count(keyword_mentions_all.id)` at 182,137 ms (the 2026-10-16 diagnostics, Z1; the same process recorded the figures route's `count(*)` at 55,310 ms). That process's store is ENCRYPTED (`at_rest_state: "unlocked-encrypted"` in the same bundle's performance and benchmark members; 6.7 GB file, 16 KiB pages, 2 CPUs, 6.8 GB RAM), so no plaintext-to-SQLCipher factor applies to it. Measured 2026-10-26 on a synthetic 12 M-row `keyword_mentions` table (plaintext SQLite, this sandbox, two runs, caches dropped): `count(distinct article_id)` 2.2 and 2.6 s, `count(id)` 1.6 and 1.9 s, so the distinct scan is 1.2 to 1.4 times the plain count cold; warm they are equal (0.36 to 0.61 s). RC2's re-measure (`rc-diagnostics/remeasure-a002-2026-10-26.md`, the app's own SQLCipher opener, 12 M and 36 M rows, cold and second pass): encrypted over plaintext 1.4 to 1.8 times cold and up to 2.4 times on the CPU-bound second pass; distinct over `count(id)` 1.2 to 1.7 times on the encrypted store; linear in rows (12 M to 36 M is 2.8 to 3.5 times). **Only the RATIOS transfer: RC2's absolute seconds are about 3 times better than this sandbox's (0.7 s against 2.2 s for the same plaintext statement), a fast virtual disk against the field's, and 182 s is not explained by decrypt CPU on either machine; it is disk and contention.** So the distinct scan's field worst is INFERRED (not measured) at 1.4 x 182 = 255 s, 310 s at the 1.7 worst ratio. The 400 s deadline is 1.6 times the first and 1.3 times the second; if RC2 or the field shows a distinct scan past it, the scan ends as a named, retried failure (`index_counts_state: "failed"`), never a wrong number, and the constant moves. The failure window 1,200 s is three of the first deadlines. At the field's 55 s `count(*)` the pair would take about 130 s (inferred) and be carried about 22 min; at the 182 s worst it is about 440 s and 73 min. So the "N to index" ticker there is a snapshot up to that old, labelled by `index_counts_computed_at` and a hover note, not a live counter.

**Rules it gives.**
1. A cache key that moves on every write is not a cache for a store that is written continuously; before relying on one, ask how often the key changes during the busiest work the app does, not at rest. The cheap part (index-backed totals) can follow the write counter on an interval; the part that costs a full scan follows its own measured cost.
2. A test that pins "a write bumps the key" pins the defect when the key is that coarse. The three tests of the retired key (`_status_cache_key`, `_noprobe_key`, the cross-connection checks) were replaced, not weakened: the contract is now "visible within the interval, with its age stated" (`tests/test_insights_status_served.py`).
3. A clock test of a carry window must move BOTH clocks the route uses AND the cache's change probe: with nothing written `served_cache` re-stamps a stale entry as verified and starts no refresh, so advancing time alone passes for the wrong reason (rule 5 of the 2026-10-16 lesson). The test moves the epoch clock, the monotonic clock the scans are timed with (a seam, `_status_clock`) and writes a row between polls; five mutations (window never honoured, cold start inline, no failure window, no deadline, interval 0) each turn a named test red.
4. The unknown is `null` plus a named state in the payload AND in the page: a `null` painted through a counter animation reads "0 indexed", a zero standing for "not known". The page reuses the Library tile's already-translated words ("counting…", "not counted") and its hover notes, so no new chrome string and the locales stay at 100 %.

**Limits, stated.** After the reindex loop finishes, the page's own header shows the exact count from the reindex response, and the next 6 s poll repaints the carried (older) `remaining` until the next pass; the hover note says when it was counted. The first poll after boot shows "counting…" for the scan figures, for a few seconds on a small corpus. A second server on the same data folder has its own cache and its own scans (several servers per data folder keep their own module state). The 2026-10-16 lesson said "only the insights routes wrap `_deadlined`"; this route is an insights route that did not, until now (that sentence is corrected there).
