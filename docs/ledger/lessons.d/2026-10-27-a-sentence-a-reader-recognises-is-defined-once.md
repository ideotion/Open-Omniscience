## 2026-10-27 — A sentence that a reader recognises is defined once and the reader is built from it; scan the code for the copy that would drift (R317)

**What was found.** The kill switch's refusal, `network kill switch is active -- collection stopped by operator`, was a string literal at five raise sites in `src/ingest/__init__.py` (`declared_sitemaps`, `fetch`, `_guard_target`, `_guarded_redirect_get`, and the robots read R297 added) and one in `src/safety/fetcher.py` (`GuardedSession.request`, a `NetworkBlocked`). Its readers each held their own copy of the opening words: the first alternative of `fetch_verdict._OFFLINE_RE` (read by the collector's `offline` bucket, the feed verification in `src/events/feeds.py`, the markets fetch in `src/markets/csv_feeds.py` and the health summary in `src/api/monitoring.py`; the preflight joins them with #1570) and `src/law/track.py`'s `_KILL_SWITCH_REFUSAL`. A rewording at one writer would have left a reader matching a sentence nobody raises any more, and the failure runs in the one direction this ledger keeps meeting: the operator's own refusal read as the HOST's failure (R293 the law tracker, R297 the robots cache, R298 the classifiers, and the preflight in #1570, which is not on main yet). Nothing would have failed loudly.

**Rules.**
1. **One definition, and the reader derived from it.** `fetch_verdict.KILL_SWITCH_REFUSAL` is the sentence and `KILL_SWITCH_REFUSAL_HEAD` its opening words (everything before ` -- `); every writer says the name, and the regex's first alternative is built from the head with `re.escape`. The reader anchors on the HEAD, not the whole sentence, because a caller may append its own detail (`src/wiki/stream.py` writes `network kill switch is active (airplane mode) -- ...`) and because the pattern's `\b` and start anchor (R298) must not move. The constant lives in `fetch_verdict.py` because that module imports only `re` (its own docstring: unit-testable anywhere); `src/ingest/__init__.py` and `src/safety/fetcher.py` import it from there (so `import src.ingest` now loads `fetch_verdict` as well, a module of its own that imports only `re`: about a millisecond), and `src/law/track.py` imports it inside `track_document` with its other `src.ingest` imports, so the law module's import time did not change.
2. **A refactor of a reader is proved to be one.** The test keeps a frozen copy of the pre-change pattern and compares `bool(match)` of the old and the new on 20 texts: the sentence bare, led by a class name, led by spaces, upper-cased, truncated, with a suffix, as a prefix of another word, quoted inside a 503 text and a redirect target, the socket guard's sentence in its three shapes, a real DNS failure, a raw `HTTPSConnectionPool(...)` text, the proxy-shaped stop of rule 6, an empty string. Flags are compared too. The anchored form is therefore pinned by comparison, not by trusting that a rewritten line is the same line. The text for "a prefix of another word" must put a WORD CHARACTER right after the head (`network kill switch is actively refusing`): `...is activated` never reaches the `\b` after `active` (`activ-a-ted` is not `active` plus more letters), so a reader that lost its word boundary passed the first version of this test. The comparison is on the compiled pattern; the predicate's own body is covered by `tests/test_fetch_verdict.py`.
3. **Scan the code, not the text, for the copy that would drift.** The test parses every file under `src/` and finds string and bytes constants, case-folded, holding the tail of the sentence (`collection stopped by operator`); the one allowed holder is the definition. Docstrings are left out of the scan (`_string_constants` skips each module, class and function docstring), so the comments and docstrings that legitimately QUOTE the sentence (this lesson, the R297 comments) do not trip it, and searching for the tail rather than the whole sentence catches a near-copy that dropped a word. A JS, HTML, JSON or YAML file under `src/` is searched for the tail as well. A second check scans EVERY file under `src/` for the opening words (`kill switch is active`) and compares the holders with a named list: the definition and the two files that hold the words for a sentence of their own (`src/wiki/ores.py`, `src/wiki/stream.py`), each with its reason in the test. The first version checked three named files only, which is blind to a fourth reader, the exact shape `src/law/track.py` had. What the scan cannot see: a sentence assembled from pieces (`+`, an f-string placeholder in the middle). A reader of the diff catches those.
4. **Drive every writer; do not match them by name.** The six writers are different code paths with different reach: `_guard_target` and `_guarded_redirect_get` refuse only on a real session, `_get_robots` raises it only when the switch is set after the earlier checks passed, `GuardedSession.request` raises a `NetworkBlocked` and not a `FetchFailed`. The test engages the switch and calls each (with the robots read reached the way R297's tests reach it) and asserts the message IS the constant, is recognised by `is_kill_switch_refusal` and classified `offline`.
5. **Seventeen mutants, each red, and one that must stay green** (applied one at a time to the working tree and restored): a re-literalised site in `fetch`, a shortened sentence at another site, a literal and a drifting copy in `safety/fetcher.py`, the reader keeping a misspelt literal of its own, the reader losing its start anchor, the reader losing its word boundary, the head reworded in the definition only, the law tracker keeping a private copy, the robots-read site drifting, `declared_sitemaps` raising a bare message, the widening of the reader's second alternative to the proxy-shaped stop (rule 6), a new module keeping the head, a capitalised copy and a bytes copy of the tail in another module, a JS file carrying the sentence, and the law tracker moved onto the wider predicate. The one that stays green is a docstring quoting the sentence. A mutant runner that edits one file twice must save the file ONCE, before the first edit: saving it again before the second edit restores the first mutant into the tree.
6. **The reader is NOT widened for a Stop through an HTTP proxy (R320).** OSM measured that a Stop through a proxy reaches a caller as `request error for <url>: ('Connection aborted.', AirplaneModeError(...))`, which `is_kill_switch_refusal` does not match. That is the wrapped shape the start anchor (R298) exists to refuse (the same words can sit inside a remote party's text), so the pattern stays as it was and is pinned: the text is in the 20-text comparison and has a test of its own (`is_kill_switch_refusal` False, `classify_fetch_failure` not `offline`). The callers that can meet it read `kill_switch_active()` after each check, the way the preflight does.
7. **Not a subclass, and why (OSM's reason, adopted as R317).** A `FetchFailed` subclass cannot replace the text match: most callers classify STORED strings (verdict JSON, queue state, diagnostics zips) and the wrapped shapes carry the sentence inside another exception's text (`request error for <url>: ... airplane mode is engaged ...`). An `isinstance` check could only ever be a fast path on top of the text match, so it was left out.
8. **Not changed, stated.** `src/law/track.py` still tests `startswith(KILL_SWITCH_REFUSAL_HEAD)` and not `is_kill_switch_refusal`: the predicate would also read the socket guard's two wrapped shapes as a refusal after the switch is cleared again, which is a widening of R293's behaviour and a decision of its own. The narrowing is pinned (a wrapped shape with the switch clear still stamps the back-off; the fetcher's own sentence restores the stamp), so moving the tracker onto the predicate is a test edit made on purpose. `src/wiki/stream.py` and `src/wiki/ores.py` keep their own sentences (`... (airplane mode) -- {doing} was refused`, `the network kill switch is active (airplane mode)`): they are not the fetcher's refusal, and the first still reads as one by the head, exactly as before.
