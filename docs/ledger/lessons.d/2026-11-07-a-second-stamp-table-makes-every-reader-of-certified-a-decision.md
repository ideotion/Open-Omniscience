## 2026-11-07 — when a table of derived rows gets its OWN stamp, every reader of "certified" is a decision to re-read, and the carry takes each half on its own evidence (R415; PR #1761)

**What was found.** `article_index_stamps` certified the keyword rows AND the places and entities with one engine id and one inputs digest, so a restore that carried an article's keywords carried its places and entities on the same evidence. The keyword engine id covers the extractor modules, not the gazetteers or the www modules, so a backup made with other gazetteers (or another `WWW_INDEXER_VERSION`) carried places and entities no local pass would produce, and the backlog counted them as certified. R415 gave the places and entities their own table, `article_www_stamps` (engine = `www_engine_id()`, inputs = `www_inputs_digest(raw_content=, country=)`); the re-index PR that added it classified it "not carried" and left this half to the restore.

**The rule.** (1) A carry is per HALF: an article's keyword rows ride the keyword stamp and its places and entities ride the www stamp, and either may be present without the other, so the plan keeps one flag per half and a refusal of one half (a keyword guard, a changed title) leaves the other where its own evidence put it. The title edit is the proof: it is an input of the keywords and of no place or entity, so it now costs the keywords a re-extraction and keeps the places carried, and a differential against a re-index shows the places are the rows a re-index writes. (2) "Owes the re-index nothing" is BOTH stamps, everywhere it is read: the backlog statement (`certified`, with the www stamp joined THROUGH the keyword one so an article counts once and only when it holds both), the stand-in `lost` statement, the post-swap re-index's skip set, and the batch status written at merge (a batch is complete only when every inserted article was carried COMPLETELY; `carried.articles` now means that, with `keyword_articles` and `www_articles` beside it). Reading any one of them from one stamp hides the other half for good: a keyword-only article would never get its places, a www-only one never its keywords. (3) The www digest is the re-index PR's function, called on the incoming row (the content column as stored, not decompressed, and the country as the column type hands it) and never recomputed in the restore. (4) A second stamp table is a second place for the rowid-reuse hazard: the merge deletes both tables' stamps for the article ids it has just inserted.

**What changed.** `src/backup/merge.py`: `_plan_derived_carry` walks the two stamp tables by one cursor each (`_stamp_pages`: one primary-key seek per page of 500, over the STAMPS and not over the articles, so a stamp whose article the backup does not hold cannot end the walk) and merges the two streams by article id (`_stamped_candidates`), verifies each half separately (`inputs_changed`, new `www_inputs_changed`; a stamp whose article the backup does not hold is counted as `article_missing`, so every candidate is accounted for), reports `www_engine`, `www_stamped`, `www_verified`, `www_planned` and a `www_reason` when the half cannot be carried at all (the backup predates the stamp, this corpus has no table for it, or the www identity cannot be computed); the keyword guards take only the keyword half; `_carry_derived_rows` writes mentions, counters and the keyword stamp for the keyword half and places, entities and the www stamp for the other, in the same window; the readers above; the registry moves `article_www_stamps` from not-carried to handled.

**A walk lesson, from the read.** The first walk merged the two tables inside ONE ordered, limited statement (a UNION with ORDER BY and LIMIT). SQLite has to read the next row of BOTH sides to return a row of either, so a table with nothing left after the cursor was scanned to its end on every page: 18 s at a million articles when one engine had changed (the common case this PR exists for), 0.15 s for the single-table walk it replaced, and growing with the square of the corpus. The plan looked fine (`MERGE (UNION)` of two rowid range searches); only timing the statement with one side empty showed it. Merge streams in the program, one seek per page per table.

**Pinned by** `tests/test_derived_carry.py` (about 22 new tests, each in the alpha-2 and alpha-3 forms, and the differential now compares the www stamps too; the existing tests moved to two stamps) and `tests/test_stand_in_rows.py`. The production skip set is pinned with an article holding the www stamp alone (the differential helper writes its own certified query, so it cannot see that rule), a stamp-implies-rows check runs on the merge-only states, and a statement-count test pins one seek per page per table whatever the other table holds. 41 mutants, 40 killed (each half carried for the other half's articles, either stamp not written or written with the wrong inputs, either stamp read alone by the backlog, the lost count, the re-index skip or the batch status, the walk over one table only, with the halves swapped, repeating a row at a page edge, needing an empty page or ending after its first, the engine compared as a prefix or not filtered, the rowid-reuse delete for one table only, the keyword guards taking the www half, the www digest called with the decompressed text or without the country, and the counts); the one survivor is equivalent (resolving the incoming keyword ids only for the keyword-carried articles saves work, and every later statement that reads them is already limited to those articles).
**Stated limits.** The first restore after the re-index PR refuses every keyword stamp made by the earlier engine id once (its own R319 line), and a backup with no places-and-entities stamp carries the keywords alone and re-extracts the places and entities. The backlog read costs one more primary-key probe per stamped pending article: about 20 to 30 ms more at 1,000,000 pending articles on this machine (the figures are in the `reindex_backlog_resume` docstring in `src/api/backup_v2.py`). An article carried for one half still goes through a full-scope re-index after the swap (`reindex_imported_articles` re-indexes anything not certified by both stamps); the store has `scope="www"` (places and entities only), which could serve an article carried for its keywords alone, and the other direction cannot use `scope="keywords"` (it leaves dates and sentiment alone). That per-half scope is a stated follow-up, not built here, and after the first restore on the new engine the half-stamped case is rare. Not seen in the field: driven by tests.
