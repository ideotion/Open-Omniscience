## 2026-10-17 — The holder's kernel state is sampled where it can be, journaled where it is cheap, and its width is paid by a measured maximum (PR #1421)

**What was found.** The 16 Oct holds of 30 to 182 s had a collector thread as holder, seven of them at a plain `SELECT` (which cannot wait for the write lock in WAL mode). The journal could not say whether the thread was working, waiting for a CPU, waiting for the GIL or a lock, sleeping in SQLite's busy handler or waiting for the disk: the holder's CPU time is read at the START and END of a hold, and a counter says how much a thread waited, not for what. The kernel does: `/proc/self/task/<tid>/{stat,wchan,schedstat}`.

**The rules.**
1. **Count the looks, classify by the kernel's own words, keep none of them.** `src/monitoring/holder_state.py` looks at the holder once a second while a hold is past `HOLD_RECORD_S` and already in the journal, and counts six classes in a fixed order (`run`, `futex`, `nap`, `disk`, `other`, `unknown`) beside `gap`, the longest time between two looks that could be read, and `rq`, the seconds waited for a CPU (rule 10). The thread's name (the kernel's `comm`, which sits in `stat`) and the `wchan` symbol are read to classify and never stored; an error is a fixed reason, never its text. `other` (a wait the kernel named otherwise) and `unknown` (a sleep it printed nothing for) are counted, not folded into a class they were not seen to be.
2. **The first look counts nothing.** It is where `rq` starts (it was the baseline of the running/runnable split before that was dropped, rule 10), and the counts keep starting at the second look so their reading did not change. A hold's counts therefore start at its second look and at the watchdog's first sight of it (30 to 45 s in), which the method says.
3. **A sampler that runs in the process it measures changes what it measures, and the record must say which way.** Every read gives the GIL up. A holder that is the only thread waiting for it is handed it by the look and read `running` (a Python spinner beside the look read `running` 5 of 5); with many waiters it usually still reads `futex`. So `futex` is a FLOOR for waits on the GIL, and the method and the module say so; the test pins that a spinner is never read asleep and does not claim it reads `futex`. `nap` means SQLite's busy handler only because the holder of the gate does not call `time.sleep` (the same syscall), which the method states.
4. **A figure the OS cannot give is a named reason, and a reason must be cleared when the counts come.** Off Linux the hold carries `ws: null` and `ws_why: unsupported-os` (the looks stop: they can never be taken); a file that vanished is `thread-gone`, one that cannot be read `proc-unreadable`; a hold with none says `not-recorded`. The journal MERGES updates, so a reason written while the files were unreadable would outlive the counts that followed: the counts carry `ws_why: null`, and a recovered hold with no count yet says `not-recorded` rather than keeping the old reason.
5. **Look often, journal rarely.** The look is 29 microseconds of CPU, once a second. Journaling it once a second would rewrite a file of up to 125,000 bytes every second, on the disk the record exists to diagnose. The counts live in memory under a leaf lock; the 15-second full tick and the release (on the holder's own thread) carry them.
6. **A width the budget counts is a measured maximum, pinned against the code.** The widest-journal model wrote each of a hold's three frames at the journal's 160-character cut. A frame is `module:function:line`; the longest any function under `src/` can produce is 79 characters. `writer.FRAME_CHARS` (79, the same number) is what the watchdog writes at most (a longer one ends in the journal's ellipsis) and what the model uses: 3 x 81 x 20 = 4,860 characters no real event could reach (4,740 bytes once the model writes a frame in its cut form, the ellipsis being three bytes). It was 90 in the first cut, eleven characters nothing had measured: a width is a measured maximum, so `tests/test_holder_state.py` walks every module's names, the PEP 695 generic-parameter scopes included, and fails BOTH ways, naming the function when one is longer or not ASCII and the constant when it is wider than any of them. The new fields cost 57 bytes a hold at their widest (1,140 over 20), 14 more for the `ws_why` null a reason leaves behind when counts follow it (280), and 827 in the method (the fixed part, defined in the budget test, 5,176 to 6,003); the widest journal went from 126,242 (5 of 20 splits at the row) to 123,749 with all 20 and 1,251 free.
7. **When a budget gets slack, the tests of its cut rule lose their premise.** Four tests assumed "the widest journal is over the row"; they now build a row `_SHORT_OF_THE_WIDEST` below the real widest member, and one asserts it really cuts.
8. **A test that counts samples of a shared singleton is exposed to the process's own background thread.** `OO_WRITE_GATE_WATCHDOG=0` in a fixture only stops a watchdog from STARTING; the one armed by an earlier test keeps ticking every 15 s on the module's gate, and a tick that lands in a 1.2 s hold adds a sample (the 17 Oct battery read `{"execute": 3}` for two ticks, about 8 per cent of runs). A 0.5 s background watchdog makes it fail every time, which is how it was reproduced; the fix lets only the test's own thread tick.
9. **The member's own prose is part of the member, and another test may have chosen a word for a sentinel.** The method sentence first said "its holder thread"; `tests/test_storage_events_runs.py` plants a secret in a text that begins with the word "holder" and asserts that word never reaches the member, so three of its cases failed on a word nothing was wrong with. The sentence says "writer" now, the same length, so no byte figure moved. The 63 new tests could not see it; the 104-file battery over the neighbouring test files did, which is what the battery is for after a change to any text the member carries.
10. **A count of looks is not a share of time, and what the kernel prints no name for is its own class.** Measured with the real look (one a second, two cores, spinners beside the holder, about 40 s, `schedstat` first-to-last as the truth): a holder on a CPU 48 per cent of the time read `R` in 17 per cent of its looks; one that waited for a CPU 12 per cent of the time and ran 1 per cent read `R` in 33 per cent; the OSM gate lab saw 77 per cent against 28. And 22 to 34 per cent of the looks of three of five scenarios were a sleep with `wchan` `0` (a fetch holder read `poll_schedule_timeout` 39 of 39, so the symbols that exist are distinct). So the running/runnable split (an inference from deltas) went, the state `R` is one class `run`, `unknown` is its own class beside `other`, and the time is a figure the scheduler gives exactly: `rq`, the second field of `schedstat` from the first look that read it to the last, to 0.1 s, null and never 0 when fewer than two looks could read it. The class change costs no bytes (still six counts), `rq` 13 bytes a hold at the widest (260 over 20) and 242 in the method: the widest journal is 124,251 bytes with 749 free, the widest hold event 1,222 bytes, both pinned like `FRAME_CHARS`.
