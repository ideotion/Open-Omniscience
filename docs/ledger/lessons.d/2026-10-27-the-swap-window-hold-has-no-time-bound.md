## 2026-10-27 — a hold cannot be bounded by a timer when its body runs calls nothing can cancel: the swap window's bounds are the wait and the file-step budget, its one data-dependent term is the last close's write-back, and a measurement of that term needs a log to write back

**What was found (the A-029 / R306 (1) design question, ruled R326 option A: no time bound, the code says so).** `library_generation.swap_window` bounds how long a swap WAITS for the window (`WINDOW_WAIT_S` = 60 s, then `LibraryBusy`) and nothing about how long it HOLDS it. The bodies that hold it are three, and their bounds differ:

| who | body | bounded by | not bounded |
|---|---|---|---|
| restore (`backup.merge.swap_in_corpus`, `merge.py:382`) | `dispose_engine()`, `disk_changing()`, `_clear_stale_side_files`, `_replace_live_corpus` | the two file steps share one deadline, `_SWAP_HANDLE_WAIT_S` = 300 s (`OO_SWAP_HANDLE_WAIT_S`), looked at after an attempt has failed, and only a lock is retried (a Windows sharing violation, error 32 or 33): elsewhere nothing is retried and the budget bounds nothing; every eighth retry calls `dispose_engine` again | the first `dispose_engine()`; every single attempt (an unlink, an `os.replace`, a re-dispose) |
| repair (`repair._swap` → `swap_in_corpus` with `check=written_to_since`) | the same plus one data-version read (`_Probe.moved()`: `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` on a connection opened with a 30 s busy timeout, `repair._probe_open`; it runs before the 300 s starts; a failure is a `Declined`) | the same 300 s for the file steps; the read by its own 30 s | the same |
| encrypt (`encrypt_tool._library_window`, `encrypt_tool.py:130`) | `disk_changing()`, two unlinks, one `os.replace` | nothing waits: a sharing violation raises at once | a call that never returns |
| a connection open behind the window | `connecting()` → `shared()` in steps of `_RECHECK_S` = 1 s | a step, deliberately not a timeout | the whole hold |

**Why a timer cannot be added.** (1) The body runs on its caller's thread inside `os.replace`, `os.unlink` and a SQLite close; a timer thread can set a flag nobody reads until the call returns. (2) Giving the window back while the body still runs is the interleaving the window exists to prevent: a connection opened between the removal of the live `-wal`/`-shm` and the replace binds the new file's log names to the old inode. (3) Failing the waiting opens early (a timeout in `connecting()`) would turn every request during a legitimate long hold into an error on a number nobody measured, and the 300 s is long on purpose (a ten-minute import is not thrown away); `_RECHECK_S` is a step and not a timeout for the same reason.

**The one term of the hold that depends on data, measured.** `dispose_engine()` closes the pool's idle connections, and SQLite writes the log back into the file when the LAST connection on it closes. RC2's row 24 had measured `dispose_engine()` at 0.0 to 0.001 s inside a window on the generator store, whose journal mode the generator turns off (RC2's report calls it a rollback-journal file, row 25): it had no log to write back, so it measured nothing about this term. The probe (the WAL lane's `swap-window-hold-data/probe.py`, outside the repository) builds a log of N MiB on the pool's one connection (`wal_autocheckpoint=0`, so every frame is still to be copied, the worst shape), returns the connection to the pool and times `with swap_window(source): dispose_engine()` through the app's own engine, with a ticker thread (10 ms) and a waiter thread that opens a connection 0.2 s into the window. One fresh process per cell. 4-core virtual disk, nothing else of ours running; plain = SQLite 3.45.1, encrypted = SQLCipher 4.12.0 on SQLite 3.51.1. Warm = the cell starts right after the log was written (its pages may be dirty); cold = `os.sync()` then `posix_fadvise(DONTNEED)` on the log and the file. The log file was absent or under 0.05 MiB afterwards in every cell (`wal_after_mib` is rounded to 0.1 MiB), and the pool's connection was idle, not checked out, when the dispose began (`checked_out` 0, read just before the dispose).

| log MiB | plain warm s | encrypted warm s | plain cold s | encrypted cold s |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.001 | 0.001 | | |
| 100 | 0.878 | 0.981 | | |
| 300 | 2.173 and 1.864 | 2.593 and 2.130 | 0.709 | 0.671 |
| 1,000 | 8.115 | 7.076 | 6.617 | 5.564 |

The hold grows with the log: warm, 6 to 10 ms a MiB at every size (0.878 s for 100 MiB plain, 8.115 s for 1,000 MiB, 9.2 times the time for 10 times the log); the two cold cells (300 MiB 0.7 s, 1,000 MiB 6.6 s, one each) are not proportional and the cause is not isolated. n is one or two a cell. **Plain: the window is held and the other threads run** (the ticker's worst gap 0.010 to 0.016 s in every cell; the waiter's open stood behind the window for the hold less its 0.2 s head start, to within 0.003 s in every plain cell: 0.68 s at 100 MiB, 1.97 and 1.67 s at 300 MiB, 7.92 s at 1,000 MiB, warm). **Encrypted: the whole process stands still for the hold**: in the six encrypted cells with a log the ticker's worst gap equals the dispose to within 0.009 s (2.601 against 2.593 s; 7.085 against 7.076 s); at 0 MiB the gap, 0.010 s, is the ticker's own resolution, because `sqlcipher3`'s `close()` keeps the GIL for the checkpoint it runs on the last connection (`shutdown_checkpoint.py` says so); the waiter's 0.15 to 0.23 s there is about the cost of an ordinary encrypted open (0.161 s with no log), so it ran after the window had ended (inferred; the order was not recorded) and the waiter column says nothing about the gate for an encrypted store. **Not reproduced:** the field's 90.8 s for a 1.41 GB log at one unlock (`shutdown_checkpoint.py`): that was the next open paying the write-back, another operation as well as another disk, at about 65 ms a MiB against 2 to 10 across the cells here.

**Why the window should find almost no log.** The restore's `_checkpoint_before_swap` returns True only when the checkpoint's answer shows every frame in the file (a False is a refusal), and the repair's `_empty_the_log` retries for `FOLD_RETRY_S` = 30 s and then declines `writer-busy`; the repair also disposes the engine before its window (`repair.py:1251`). So the window should find only the frames written between the fold and the window. How many a real restore leaves there is NOT measured.

**Not covered:** a log written under load; 1.4 GB or more; a real restore or repair end to end (the cells call `dispose_engine()` as the body); the encrypt body; Windows (sharing violations, antivirus holds); a store with a log and other pooled connections (a straggler leaves the log; `dispose.out` records it). Two hand-run 100 MiB cells (1.08 s plain, 0.79 s encrypted, printed and not saved) are not used.

**The rules.**

1. **Ask "is this hold bounded" per body, and split it into what is bounded by a budget, what is bounded by a step and what is bounded by nothing.** The window's wait, the file-step budget and the connection opens' step are three different bounds on three different things, and "the hold has no timer" is true of the body and false of the restore's file steps.
2. **A measurement of a data-dependent term needs the data.** A `dispose_engine()` timed on a store with no log says nothing about a log; the cell has to contain the thing the term depends on (here, a leftover log on the last connection) and say what it contained.
3. **Do not put a timer on a hold whose body is uninterruptible, and do not release it early.** Bound the wait, bound the retries, record what is not bounded and why. The one place the code itself can still leave the window held for good is the call that gives it back (the R199 residual, 0.5.1 item 24); a stopped pool there is expected to be loud (the pool's waits and `pool_timeout`; the MemoryError report reasoned this and did not measure it), and a detector that names it, as `kv_store._refuse_if_stuck` does for the settings lock (decide by where the owner is, not for how long), is a proposal and not built.
4. **On an encrypted store a long close is not a held gate, it is a stopped process.** `sqlcipher3`'s `close()` keeps the GIL, so a hold made of a close is invisible to any thread that would measure or wait for it. Say so wherever a figure for it is given, and never read a waiter's latency in such a cell as the gate's.
5. **Options named so they are not re-asked:** a timeout in `connecting()` (rejected: every request errors during a legitimate long hold, on an unmeasured number); a log line or storage event recording the hold (not now: no report asks for it, the A-029 precedent is "build when a real report shows it"); a stuck-hold detector (not built, rule 3); disposing before the window in `swap_in_corpus` as the repair does (not built: the benefit needs a writer to commit between the fold and the window, which is unmeasured, and `merge.py` is another lane's file). A-029 itself (the lifespan disposing the engine while job threads may still write) is unchanged: leave, keep the `dispose.out` record.
