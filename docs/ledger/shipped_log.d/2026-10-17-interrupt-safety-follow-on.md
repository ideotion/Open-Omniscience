## 2026-10-17 — interrupt-safety follow-on (PR #1426)

**Before:** after the gate swap (R144, #1416) two kinds of interrupt damage were still reproducible with real signals on CPython 3.13: a wait loop written `while wanted:` under a lock left the lock owned in 0.7 to 0.9 % of interrupts; and a context manager that took shared state before its `try` left it taken (`swap_window` left its holder or stamp set in 7,013 of 30,872 interrupts). The loops a CLI reaches on the main thread, and the claim that the others are worker-only, rested on reports.
**After:** every main-thread-reachable wait loop has the covered shape and a dis test; the exempt ones are pinned to their callers by a by-name call graph (`tests/test_worker_only_callers_pinned.py`); R171's scan finds every context manager that takes before its try (29: 4 converted, `swap_window` converted, 2 owed, 23 allowlisted with a reason). Full suite at 46ce6e548 (this branch plus a settings-lock detector that is not in this PR), 2026-10-17, started 06:40 UTC, 4 xdist workers, host otherwise idle: 23,587 passed, 214 skipped, 4 failed in 26 min 30 s; the four: test_gzip_flushes_every_chunk_of_a_streaming_response (fails on origin/main too, starlette below 1.5.0), the two tests/test_event_loop_signal_exemption.py tests that boot a real server and wait 60 s for it to be healthy (not healthy in 60 s with 4 workers running; both pass alone in 34 s), and one test of the detector's own.
