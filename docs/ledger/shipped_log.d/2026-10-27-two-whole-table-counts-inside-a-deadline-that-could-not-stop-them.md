## 2026-10-27 — two whole-table counts that sat inside a statement deadline which could not stop them now loop: `count(id)` for the Library figures' `keyword_mentions` count and `count(1)` for the content-share estimate (deadline-bound sweep S1 and S2, R309; the bundle recording that added S6 and the S7 stated limit, R312; PR #NNNN)

**What was found.** `statement_deadline` is a SQLite progress handler asked once per 20,000 virtual-machine opcodes, and a bare `count(*)` is one opcode with the whole b-tree walk inside it, so the handler is never asked while it runs and a deadline around it bounds nothing (the finding of #1543, for `fts_status`). The WAL lane swept every `statement_deadline` block in `src/` (`wal-lane/deadline-bound-sweep-2026-10-27.md`; every `wal-lane/` path in this entry is a file in the project's shared folder, not in the repo): it recorded every statement executed while a block was open during the test suite (2,557 distinct (call site, statement) shapes from 23 of the 58 call sites), read the opcodes of each, then ran the suspects under the REAL deadline at a millionth of a second on a synthetic store of 300,000 articles and 1,240,657 mentions, opened through the app's own SQLCipher 4.12.0 engine (SQLite 3.51.1), warm. Eight recorded shapes compile to a single `OP_Count`; two sit in files this change owns:

- **S1, `src/api/database.py` (`_mentions_count`).** The Library figures' background count of `keyword_mentions_all` was `SELECT count(*)`. Its 300 s deadline is documented as protecting the pooled connection and the read snapshot the count holds (an open read pins the WAL from checkpointing). Under 1e-6 s it ran whole (handler asked 0 times); `count(id)` stopped (asked 186 times). THE HOLD IT SHORTENS: the connection and the WAL-pinning snapshot the count holds lasted as long as the count did, and are now cut at the deadline. It is one of the long holds the A-029 swap-window question concerns (`swap_window` has no timer on the hold, `WINDOW_WAIT_S` bounds only the wait to get it); this change makes the 300 s real for this count and proposes no bound for the window.
- **S2, `src/monitoring/storage.py` (`content_share_estimate`).** Neither qualified sqlcipher3 build (0.6.2, 0.6.4) has `dbstat`, so this sampled estimate is the encrypted store's NORMAL path, not a fallback; its docstring said it was "bounded by the statement deadline" and it began with a bare `count(*) FROM articles`. The 400 probes after it are short statements: the handler's 20,000 opcodes accumulate across the statements of a connection, and 400 statements of about 30 opcodes ran whole under a 1e-6 s deadline (49 ms, `SELECT id, length(v) FROM t WHERE id >= :p ORDER BY id LIMIT 1` on a 5,000-row plain table, SQLite 3.45.1; 4,000 raised), and with the count respelled bare the whole estimate came back complete under that deadline. So the loop is bounded by its own count, which the docstring now says.
- **S6, `src/monitoring/benchmark.py` (`_store_and_corpus`), added after the first cut.** The scaling benchmark's corpus context counted `keyword_mentions` with a bare `SELECT COUNT(*)`. RC2 found it while building the S3 test for `performance.py`; the sweep had listed it with the words "no deadline around it", which was true of the function and false of its caller: `benchmark.json` is a member of the all-diagnostics bundle and the bundle wraps every database-touching member in `statement_deadline(db, <300 s>)` (`bundle.py:2921`). The sweep missed it because its recording was the test suite's statements and the suite never runs that member under the bundle's block; the entry was classified by reading the function, not the call chain. It is `count(1)` now (`tests/test_benchmark_corpus_count_deadline.py`, 5 tests, the real deadline at 1e-6 over 30,000 mentions, red against the bare spelling); `run_benchmark` already reports a context failure as `corpus.error`.

**What changed.** `_mentions_count` is `select(func.count(KeywordMentionRead.id))` (`id` is the view's primary key and never NULL, so the number is the same); `content_share_estimate` counts with `count(1)`; the docstring and the comments say what each deadline bounds (the aggregated `dbstat('main', 1)` walk is one `xNext` the handler cannot interrupt on plain SQLite, 555 ms free against 545 ms under 1e-6, while its `GROUP BY` form stops; the comment at the figures deadline says the 43, 55 and 182 s figures were measured on the bare statement and are "superseded, pending"). No key, shape or label changed. `tests/test_deadline_bound_statements.py` (8 tests): the shipped statements under the real deadline at 1e-6 over 30,000 rows, the background count's named failure through `_compute_figures_counted` with no fake count, the same number as the bare count on 0, 1, 1,000 and 30,000 mentions (non-zero where it can be), and the estimate completing under a generous deadline. Each bound test was checked red against its statement respelled as a bare `count(*)`.

**Stated limits, no PR (four; the first three are the sweep's numbers, 300,000 articles, SQLCipher 4.12.0, warm).**
1. **An FTS5 phrase iterator's set-up is not interruptible.** `SELECT rowid FROM article_fts WHERE article_fts MATCH '"w00000 w00001"' ORDER BY bm25(article_fts, 4.0, 1.0) LIMIT 20000` came back in 90 ms under a 1e-6 s deadline against a 371 ms free run (a 3-term phrase 127 ms of 189 ms); the AND form and `NEAR(...)` stopped in 18 ms. The term was in EVERY article (a worst case); the set-up scales with the postings of the phrase terms; not measured above 300,000 articles.
2. **`PRAGMA quick_check` and `integrity_check` have an uninterruptible page-walk unit.** `EXPLAIN` shows two `OP_IntegrityCk` each; the deadline's first consultation arrived after 270 to 300 ms on 975 MiB, consistent with about 0.3 ms per MiB warm for the page-structure walk, after which the row checks stop. Cold and larger files not measured.
3. **`verify.py`'s Stop answers after the handler interval, not at once.** `src/database/verify.py` asks its cancel flag every 100,000 opcodes; with a handler that returns 1 at its first consultation `PRAGMA quick_check(articles)` came back at 1,518 ms (free 3,311 ms), `quick_check(keyword_mentions)` at 1,282 ms (free 2,190 ms), the whole-file `quick_check` at 1,549 ms. `salvage.py` uses the same interval (not timed); `merge.py`'s Stop handler (1,000 opcodes) cannot interrupt a bare `COUNT(*)` inside a step (catalogue-scale tables; not timed).
4. **The six per-table counts of `database_stats` run bare inside the bundle's `performance.json` member (R312).** A second recording ran all 57 members of `_all_diagnostics_members` through `_write_all_diagnostics_zip` on a 15 MiB synthetic store (671 distinct (member, statement) shapes; 32 members touch the database and run inline under the per-member deadline at `bundle.py:2921`, the other 25 with no handler). Six bare `SELECT count(*) AS count_1 FROM <articles | sources | keywords | commodity_prices | article_links | article_mentioned_dates>` shapes sit in `performance.json`: they are `database_stats._compute` (`src/api/database.py:140`, the `_COUNTED_TABLES` loop), reached through `performance_report(selftest=True)` at `performance.py:235`. THE ATTRIBUTION IS BY STATEMENT IDENTITY, not by a call stack (the recorder keeps the stack of deadline blocks, not of calls): the six tables and their order are `_COUNTED_TABLES`, and the only other site that emits that shape, `src/api/library.py:103`, is not a bundle member. Left as a stated limit: `database_stats` is the Database tab's served-cache route with no deadline of its own, so `count(1)` there would charge every refresh the loop form's CPU to buy a stop that only the bundle member's 300 s deadline could use; the overrun a bare count can cause is one count's duration, and these tables are smaller than `keyword_mentions`. Not measured: the sizes of `article_links` and `article_mentioned_dates` on a field corpus. THE RECORDER'S OWN LIMITS: a 15 MiB store; `ai-runs.json`, `ai.json`, `country-code-duplicates.json` and `home-cards.json` executed no statement on it (not covered); a member that opens its own connection is invisible to it, because it sees only statements while a deadline block is open.
