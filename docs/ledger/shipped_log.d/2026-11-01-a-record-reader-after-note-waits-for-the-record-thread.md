## 2026-11-01 — the damage-record tests no longer lean on `note()`'s two-second wait for the record thread (PR #1691)

The Core-only job of #1688 (run 41318585353) failed one test, `tests/test_database_damage.py::test_an_incident_of_another_process_is_its_own_record_even_when_no_session_id_is_known`, on `assert 0 == 1`. `DamageRegistry.note` joins its `oo-damage-record` thread for at most `RECORD_WAIT_S` (2.0 s) and `state(detail=True)["incidents"]` does not wait at all, so a test reading the record right after its notes passed only if the thread finished inside two seconds. A plugin that slows `_record_new` to 0.3 s and the wait to 0.01 s reproduced the exact assertion on the unfixed file (27 of 107 tests failed). TEST-ONLY fix (R404, R335): `_settle(reg)` before the first read of a record job's outcome in 20 tests (25 insertions; the 20th is the very test R404 names, found by the delta read of this PR and then by a second stall plugin that slows every job's start), and the `registry` fixture settles on teardown, because the registry is one process-global object and a leftover record thread wrote 5 extra incidents into the next test (17 read where 12 were noted). 107 pass unstalled, under the stall plugin, and on SQLite 3.45.1; six mutants (each settle I added to the two named tests, and the fixture teardown, removed one at a time) are killed under the stalls. Not shown: what stalled the thread in CI (inferred: a file-system stall, a GC pause or CPU starvation) and the Core-only order itself.
