## 2026-10-26 — the wall-clock sweep: three failures on main fixed, and 48 test files that sat on the same three kinds of fragility moved to orders, observed handshakes, CPU clocks and the numbers the code asked for (test-only, PR #1524)

**What it fixes (the three failures first).**

1. `tests/test_write_gate_breakdown.py` (a session's commit bucket): the test held a bucket to a fixed figure; the bucket is now held to the clock around the statement or the commit that opened it (`held <= took + slack`), a relation that holds however long a loaded runner made the statement. Mutants: the commit bucket re-opened at its end, the statement-end event missing (3 tests), every statement sorted as an INSERT, `after_commit` never closing the bucket: each red by name.
2. `tests/test_holder_state.py` (a fourth tick, 1.68 s, in a simulation of three): the simulation patches `writer.watchdog_tick` and `writer.sample_holder_state` for every thread, and the process's own watchdog (armed by the first hold of the whole run, a wake every 15 s) resolves those names at call time; one wake inside the ~50 ms the simulation takes was recorded as a tick of the loop at the simulated time it landed on. The recorders now answer a stranger with the no-op a gate with nothing to report returns, and a new test makes a thread of the watchdog's name call both functions at a fixed point (the 40th look): without the pinning that test is red, with it the loop's ticks are its own three and the stranger got the no-op answers.
3. `tests/test_scheduler_runner.py::test_continuous_loop_runs_passes_back_to_back` (pass two never arrived inside 5 s): the cause was not a missing throttle. After each pass the loop runs `run_pass_hygiene` (trafilatura reset, a whole-process `gc.collect`, `malloc_trim`, a WAL checkpoint through the write gate, bounded at 30 s, on a 300 s cadence), `_drain_owed_custody` and `record_run`, and then `_run_off_peak_maintenance` (1.5 s on an empty database); pass two sits behind all of that. Measured, the tail is 0.5 s on a fresh process and 1.6 s with eight million objects alive, and the full run of a pull request (57 minutes) has a heap to match. `tests/wallclock_helpers.py::quiet_pass_tail` takes the tail out of the tests of the LOOP (the tail has its own tests), the wait is the 20 s hang guard, and the test that asserted the interval mode now throttles the maintenance like its siblings. Proof by injection, not by a rerun: a plugin that slows the tail by 12 s makes the old test red at 6 s and the new one green at 25 s, and with the stubs removed the new test is red again at 25 s.

The same family was found in three more places and fixed with the same helper: `test_scheduler.py::test_scheduler_start_runs_then_stops` (the result is recorded just after the count is bumped; the loop's end is observed, since `stop()` joins for a bounded time and answers True either way), `test_scheduler.py::test_scheduler_api` (the stop route joins for 10 s by default) and `test_scheduler_pause_resume.py` (joins of 5 s).

**The numbers the code asked for.** Replacing an upper bound by a 20 s hang guard keeps a test able to catch a hang and loses its power over a wait that is bounded but wrong: a wait of 2 to 5 s comes back inside the guard. **What this does not show.** The runner that failed was not observed: the mechanisms reproduced are the signatures (a slowed tail for the scheduler, a stray wake for the holder state, a budget a healthy member must fit under), not a measurement of that run. The harsh freeze stretches every requested sleep to a stop, so it overstates sleep-bound tests; a mild-freeze red is real and a harsh red is a question. **Two limits that stay.** (1) A test that holds a party past a 20 s guard tells a hang from a wait, and where there is no number to read (a read that finishes late, an inline step that costs 5 s) it cannot tell a slow call from a fast one; the old fixed bounds could, and flaked. Where the call passes its bound to a wait or a join the test reads it instead; where it does not, the order assertion is what remains. (2) The soak skips, with the writer's rate in the reason, when the writer was starved enough that the byte check could fail: on such a runner the property is not shown, which the skip says, rather than asserted.
