## 2026-10-13 — write-gate holds: the backlog drain gives the gate back (PR #1367)

- **2026-10-13 · wal-disk-pool/write-gate-holds (WAL / disk thread; crash findings 2026-09-30, causes B and C).**
With nothing collecting, the backlog drain re-indexes at `commit_batch=200` and holds the single-writer gate from a batch's first article to its commit, so any writer that arrives (a save from the UI, the Wikipedia drain) waits the whole batch: about 36 s at the 333 articles a minute the field measured on 27 Sep, 60 to 175 s on the machine that crashed. `reindex_articles` now takes `should_flush(staged, held_s)`, asked after every staged article (store.py gains that parameter and three stat keys: `batch_commits` and `early_batch_commits`, counted where a commit landed, and `longest_batch_s`, the longest a batch held the gate from the moment it took it, after its wait, to the commit or rollback that ended it, a failed commit included, and None when no batch ran so that width 1 never reads as an instant hold); `src/backup/merge.py` has `REINDEX_HOLD_TARGET_S = 10` (`OO_REINDEX_HOLD_TARGET_S`; 0 = the count-only rule, anything that is not a finite number at or above 0 keeps the default so a typo never removes the bound) and `reindex_flush_policy`, which answers True once the batch has held the gate for the target or `write_gate_waiters()` is above zero and the batch has held the gate for at least `REINDEX_QUEUED_WRITER_MIN_HOLD_S` (1 s). `commit_batch` stays the ceiling, the floor is one article, a policy that raises is dropped for the rest of the call (class name logged, never its text), and an import that owns the machine passes no policy. The drain resolves the target per batch beside the width and publishes it (`hold_target_seen`) with the new stats (longest batch is a max across batches, never a sum). `WriterGate.waiters()` / `write_gate_waiters()` are the one new gate accessor. The gate also writes `write gate released after 61.3s by 'holder'` when a hold of the watchdog threshold or more ends (outside the gate's lock; the thread name and the seconds, no exception or engine text; never for a hold under one second). Why: the watchdog warns once per hold at the first sample at or past the threshold, so the logged "60.3 to 73.3 s" of the 30 Sep crash are detection times, not lengths (a 20 s hold under a 3 s threshold logs "held 3.0s"), and the gate keeps only the process's longest hold.
Measured (synthetic store, 1,200 articles; 600 re-indexed through the app's own gated `session_scope`; a second writer asking for the gate every 0.1 s; this machine applies about 5 times faster than the field's 333 articles a minute, so the field's holds are about 5 times these): width 200 holds 7.93 s plain and 5.98 s SQLCipher and the arriving writer waits 7.89 s and 5.96 s; width 50 holds 2.04 s and 2.24 s with apply 21.1 s against 21.7 s (no measurable cost); width 10 holds 0.77 s and 0.67 s and costs 25.4 s of apply (plain) and 25.7 s (encrypted), against 21.7 s and 18.5 s, with 60 commits of about 0.1 s each. An age target of 2 s on width 200 (this machine's equivalent of 10 s) cut the longest hold to 2.34 s and 2.28 s at no apply cost; with a writer every 3 s behind the default policy the writer waited 0.67 s, and with a writer that is always queued the one-second minimum cut the first build's 166 commits per 600 articles (apply 31.7 s) to 20 (apply 22.1 s, against 20.7 s with no policy) for a 1.2 s wait. Not measured: the field machine's commit time, a real UI save and the Wikipedia drain as the arriving writer. NOT explained: the 2,415 s longest wait of crash 091717 (cause B) cannot be attributed from the findings (the gate's `max_hold_s` for that session is not in them); it waits for the next zip from the long run. Left alone and named in the queue: `ReindexJobManager` at `OO_REINDEX_COMMIT_BATCH` above 1 (its default is 1), and the one-commit-per-article path. 39 new tests (25 on the gate accessor, the target, the policy and the pass, 6 on the drain wiring, 8 on the release line); 34 mutants of the first group and 8 of the second are killed. An independent read of the first build found no block and four fixes, all folded: the hold clock now starts after the gate is taken and also covers a failed commit and a failed article, a run that timed no batch publishes None, and four test gaps closed.
