## 2026-10-27 — `fts.fts_status` takes `rows` from `count(*) FROM articles` (a covering-index scan) instead of `count(*) FROM article_fts` (a scan of every article row): 35 times cheaper cold at 1,000,000 articles, and the premise that a count of articles is a corpus scan is withdrawn (0.5.1 item 29 follow-on, R295, PR #1543)

**The defect (a clear one, found by measuring, in the WAL lane's own file).** After #1534 the WAL lane measured `fts_status` and `corpus_integrity` cold on an encrypted store of 1,000,000 articles (`wal-lane/cold-encrypted-1m-measurement-2026-10-27.md`; the app's own SQLCipher engine, 16 KiB pages, a 64 MiB cache, synthetic Latin text of 1.3 KB per article, a virtual disk reading 1.3 GB/s, cold = the files evicted from the OS page cache by `posix_fadvise` AND a fresh process, 5 runs). The new ends statement cost 76 to 100 ms cold. 99% of the call was step 3, `SELECT count(*) FROM article_fts`, which has been there since D4: 9.6 to 11.9 s cold, 5.5 to 6.0 s warm, and the same cost as a forced table scan of `articles` (`count(*) FROM articles NOT INDEXED`: 9.5 to 10.5 s cold), because FTS5 answers a `count(*)` on an external-content table by reading every row of the content table. Its deadline is 15 s, so it was 3 to 5 s from timing out at this size, and its cost follows the article TEXT: real articles are several times larger, so on a real encrypted store `rows` would have read `None` / `timed_out` at a much smaller article count (an extrapolation from the table-scan rate, not measured). The number it gives is the articles' count, which `SELECT count(*) FROM articles` gives from the covering index `idx_article_created_at` in 0.25 s cold.

**What changed.** Step 3 of `fts_status` is `SELECT count(*) FROM articles` inside the same count deadline. `rows`, `count_status`, the probe, step 4 (the index's ends and population, #1534's one statement) and every key and label are unchanged. The docstrings say what `rows` now is (the articles' count, the figure FTS5's own count returned, with the measured costs and their limits) and no longer say that a count of articles is a corpus scan: `ensure_fts`'s SCOPE paragraph states that the boot compares no counts as a decision, gives the 0.25 s measurement with its limits and says the old 74 s figure (a count on a 2.7 GB corpus, no statement recorded) was not reproduced; `fts_status`'s `index_state` bullet names `rows` against `indexed_documents` as the comparison that sees a hole, which `monitoring/integrity.py` makes as its `delta` from its own count. `monitoring/integrity.py` is not touched; its second `count(*) FROM articles` (0.2 to 0.3 s cold) now sits beside the status's, and reusing `rows` for it is a possible follow-on, not done here. One xfail `reason` in `tests/test_repair_search_index.py` repeated the premise and is reworded.

**Measured after (same store, same method, 5 cold runs; ms, min / median / max).** `fts_status` whole, cold: **292 / 327 / 558** (before: 9,729 / 11,382 / 11,975); warm: 2.1 / 2.3 / 2.8 (before 5,539 / 5,801 / 6,009). Inside it the articles' count is 217 / 240 / 288 cold and 0.7 to 1.0 warm, the ends statement 71 / 80 / 264 (one run caught it slow) and steps 1, 2 and 4's presence reads under 10. The count alone, cold: 207 / 256 / 312. `corpus_integrity` whole, cold: 11,706 / 12,607 / 14,971 (before 17,159 / 17,343 / 17,381); warm 5,866 / 5,948 / 6,314 (before 11,417 / 11,476 / 11,936). The remainder of the report is `PRAGMA foreign_key_check`: 11,075 / 12,011 / 14,151 cold here (10,879 to 11,069 in the first measurement: the same statement, a wider spread). The report now reads healthy, `rows` and `articles` and `indexed_documents` 1,000,000, `ends-agree`, `delta` 0, in all runs.

**Tests (`tests/test_fts_status.py`: 38 test items run, 7 of them new or rewritten for this change).** Rewritten, not deleted: the five that simulated the old statement (`count(*) FROM article_fts`: the slow count, the failing-probe parameter, integrity and schema drift agreeing when the count times out, which now also pins that the integrity `delta` says `None` and the index's own population is still had, the population failing beside an earlier error, the verdict not replacing the count's error) now fail `count(*) FROM articles`; and the prefix test that pinned "no statement counts the articles" (a decision of #1534 that R295 withdrew) now pins that exactly ONE does, that it is the plain `SELECT count(*) FROM articles`, that its `EXPLAIN QUERY PLAN` (taken on the statement the code issued, captured at the cursor) is a covering-index scan, and that nothing counts `article_fts` itself. New: `rows` equals the old statement and the articles' count over a full, a shrunken (two deletes and an ingest that takes a freed id), an emptied and a partial index; a partial index still reads `partial (inferred)`, `PartialIndex`, one document, with the count answered (`rows` 2) and with it timed out (`rows` None, `timed_out`); and the count runs inside a deadline of its own. One xfail reason reworded. **Red without it (each applied to the committed tree, then restored; `tests/test_fts_status.py` and the sink pin):** the old statement back: 9 tests; a count that reads a column: 1; `NOT INDEXED`: 1; `rows` read from the shadow table's count: 20; the count without its deadline: 1; the verdict waiting for the count: 3; `rows` off by one: 12; a slow count reported as an error: 3. The related suites (`test_fts*`, `test_integrity*`, `test_sink_sweep_records`, `test_repair_search_index`, `test_heavy_integrity_sweep`, `test_merge_fts_deferred`) pass in the Core-only profile: 951 passed, 2 skipped, 1 xfailed.

**Stated limits.** (1) One machine and one disk class (a virtual disk reading 1.3 GB/s; the codec dominates), synthetic Latin text of 1.3 KB per article, nothing above 1,000,000 articles: a laptop's cold figures are higher for both statements, and the scaling statements above 1 M are extrapolations. The covering-index count follows the number of articles, not their text. (2) The plan is the planner's choice, not a promise: `count(*) FROM articles` uses `idx_article_created_at` on SQLite 3.45.1 / SQLCipher 3.51.1 and the test pins it on the real schema; a store that lost every secondary index would pay a table scan again, and `count_status` would say `timed_out` rather than lie. (3) `PRAGMA foreign_key_check` (`src/monitoring/integrity.py:217`, inside the shared 60 s `statement_deadline` of `corpus_integrity`) is now 90% of the integrity report cold at this size, 11 to 14 s, on a store whose keyword tables are EMPTY: a real store's cost is larger and unmeasured. It is a separate question (a budget for the report) and was not touched. (4) The boot still compares no counts: that is a decision, left as it was; whether it may now is P2, a proposal. (5) `rows` is still the content table's count and not the index's size; the integrity `delta` and `fts.index_state` carry the index. (6) The measurement store was deleted after the figures were recorded; the scripts and raw results are in `wal-lane/m1m-data/` and `results_p1.jsonl`'s figures are in this entry.
