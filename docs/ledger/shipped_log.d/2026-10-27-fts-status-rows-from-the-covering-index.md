## 2026-10-27 — `fts.fts_status` takes `rows` from `count(1) FROM articles` (a covering-index scan the deadline can stop) instead of `count(*) FROM article_fts` (a scan of every article row): 30 times cheaper cold at 1,000,000 articles, and the premise that a count of articles is a corpus scan is withdrawn (0.5.1 item 29 follow-on, R295, PR #1543)

**The defect (a clear one, found by measuring, in the WAL lane's own file).** After #1534 the WAL lane measured `fts_status` and `corpus_integrity` cold on an encrypted store of 1,000,000 articles (`wal-lane/cold-encrypted-1m-measurement-2026-10-27.md`; the app's own SQLCipher engine, 16 KiB pages, a 64 MiB cache, synthetic Latin text of 1.3 KB per article, a virtual disk reading 1.3 GB/s, cold = the files evicted from the OS page cache AND a fresh process, 5 runs). The new ends statement cost 76 to 100 ms cold. 99% of the call was step 3, `SELECT count(*) FROM article_fts`, which has been there since D4: 9.6 to 11.9 s cold, 5.5 to 6.0 s warm, the same cost as a forced table scan of `articles` (`count(*) FROM articles NOT INDEXED`: 9.5 to 10.5 s cold), because FTS5 answers a `count(*)` on an external-content table by reading every row of the content table. Its deadline is 15 s, so it was 3 to 5 s from timing out at this size, and its cost follows the article TEXT: real articles are several times larger, so on a real encrypted store `rows` would have read `None` / `timed_out` at a much smaller article count (an extrapolation from the table-scan rate, not measured). The number it gives is the articles' count, which a count over a covering index gives in 0.24 to 0.33 s cold.

**What changed.** Step 3 of `fts_status` is `SELECT count(1) FROM articles` inside the same count deadline. `rows`, `count_status`, the probe, step 4 (the index's ends and population, #1534's one statement) and every key and label are unchanged. The docstrings say what `rows` now is (the articles' count, the figure FTS5's own count returned on a store whose indexes agree with their table, with the measured costs and their limits) and no longer say that a count of articles is a corpus scan: `ensure_fts`'s SCOPE paragraph states that the boot compares no counts as a decision, gives the measurement with its limits and says the old 74 s figure (a count on a 2.7 GB corpus, no statement recorded) was not reproduced; `fts_status`'s `index_state` bullet names `rows` against `indexed_documents` as the comparison that sees a hole, which `monitoring/integrity.py` makes as its `delta` from its own count. `monitoring/integrity.py` is not touched. One xfail `reason` in `tests/test_repair_search_index.py` (Export's test) repeated the premise and is reworded. The two same-day fragments that carry the premise (`repair-search-index-evidence`, `fts-status-index-population-evidence`) get a dated note and are otherwise verbatim.

**Why `count(1)` and not `count(*)` (the independent read's blocker, reproduced).** SQLite runs a bare `count(*)` as ONE opcode (`OP_Count`, a C loop over the b-tree). `statement_deadline` is a progress handler, which the VDBE consults only between opcodes at loop bottoms, so while a `count(*)` runs it is never consulted: over a covering index at 200,000 articles the handler (every 20,000 steps) was consulted 0 times for `count(*)`, 30 for `count(1)` and 90 for the old FTS statement; at 3,000,000 rows in memory 0, 450 and (for a `WHERE`) 600; and the app's own `statement_deadline(0.5 s)` around `count(*)` over 6,000,000 rows returned after 0.74 s with no `StatementTimeout`, where the same deadline around a row-loop count stopped at 0.50 s. So the first version of this change (`count(*)`, 292 / 327 / 558 ms cold) made `count_status: timed_out` unreachable from step 3 while its docstrings, its deadline test and this entry said the opposite. `count(1)` uses the same covering index and is a row loop the handler can stop. The same property holds for the two counts this change does not touch (see the limits).

**Measured (same method as the first measurement; `wal-lane/p1-remeasure-2026-10-27.md`, second section).** The first store had been deleted, so the final statement was measured on a store REBUILT with the same seeded generator (1,000,000 articles, the same first full-text build in 128.7 s against 129.9 s): same data, a different file. 5 cold rounds, ms, min / median / max. `fts_status` whole, cold: **294 / 373 / 488** (before, the old statement on the first store: 9,729 / 11,382 / 11,975); warm: 17.2 / 24.3 / 25.8 (before 5,539 / 5,801 / 6,009). Inside it the articles' count is 227 / 286 / 379 cold and 15.7 / 22.3 / 23.8 warm, the ends statement 62 / 78 / 101 cold. `count(1)` alone: 236 / 289 / 326 cold, 15.5 / 18.2 / 24.1 warm; `count(*)` alone as a control: 231 / 285 / 314 cold, 1.0 / 1.1 / 1.5 warm: the same cost cold (both read the index's pages), 17 ms more warm for a loop the deadline can stop. `corpus_integrity` whole, cold: 11,676 / 14,644 / 15,331 (before 17,159 / 17,343 / 17,381), warm 6,219 / 6,512 / 6,902 (before 11,417 / 11,476 / 11,936); the remainder is `PRAGMA foreign_key_check`: 10,925 / 13,925 / 14,636 cold across this run (10,879 to 11,069 and 11,075 to 14,151 in the earlier ones: the same statement, a wide spread). All runs: healthy, `rows` = `indexed_documents` = 1,000,000, `ends-agree`, `delta` 0.

**Tests (`tests/test_fts_status.py`: 39 test items run, 15 of them new or rewritten for this change).** Rewritten, not deleted: the five that simulated the old statement (`count(*) FROM article_fts`: the slow count, the failing-probe parameter, integrity and schema drift agreeing when the count times out, which now also pins that the integrity `delta` says `None` and the index's own population is still had, the population failing beside an earlier error, the verdict not replacing the count's error, twice) now fail the articles' count, through one constant (`_ARTICLES_COUNT`); and the prefix test that pinned "no statement counts the articles" (a decision of #1534 that R295 withdrew) now pins that exactly ONE does, that it is the plain `SELECT count(1) FROM articles`, that its `EXPLAIN QUERY PLAN` (taken on the statement the code issued, captured at the cursor) is a covering-index scan, and that nothing counts `article_fts` itself. New: `rows` equals the old statement and the articles' count over a full, a shrunken (two deletes and an ingest that takes a freed id), an emptied and a partial index (four items); a partial index still reads `partial (inferred)`, `PartialIndex`, one document, with the count answered (`rows` 2) and with it timed out (`rows` None, `timed_out`) (two items); the count runs inside a `statement_deadline` block given `_fts_count_deadline_s()` (not the probe's 5 s, not a number that never fires: the value is pinned); and the issued statement is re-run on the raw connection under a progress handler that asks to be consulted at every step, which must be (this is what the first version lacked: its deadline test pinned nesting, not a bound). **Red without it (each applied to the committed tree, then restored):** first version, 8 of 8 (the old statement back 9 tests; a count that reads a column 1; `NOT INDEXED` 1; `rows` from the shadow table's count 20; no deadline 1; the verdict waiting for the count 3; `rows` off by one 12; a slow count reported as an error 3). The fold, 7 of 7: `count(*)` back 9 (including the handler test); the probe's 5 s deadline 1; a deadline that never fires 1; no deadline 1; `count(1)` over the full-text table 10; a `WHERE` on the count 1 (the exact-text pin only: a `WHERE` keeps a covering-index plan); `count(content)` 10. The independent read's own 22 mutants left two alive (the deadline's value), which the value pin kills. The related suites (`test_fts*`, `test_integrity*`, `test_sink_sweep_records`, `test_repair_search_index`, `test_heavy_integrity_sweep`, `test_merge_fts_deferred`) pass in the Core-only profile: 953 passed, 2 skipped, 1 xfailed.

**Stated limits.** (1) One machine and one disk class (a virtual disk reading 1.3 GB/s; the codec dominates), synthetic Latin text of 1.3 KB per article, nothing above 1,000,000 articles: a laptop's cold figures can be higher or lower for both statements, and the scaling statements above 1 M are extrapolations. The covering-index count follows the number of articles, not their text. (2) The index the count walks is the planner's pick, and it varies with the order the schema's indexes were created in: on the measured store it was `idx_article_created_at`, about four times the pages of the smallest candidate (948 pages against 243 at 100,000 articles by `dbstat`), so the figure is conservative; the plan test pins "a covering-index scan" on the real schema, not which index. A store that lost every secondary index would still be covered by the primary-key autoindex; the cost then follows that b-tree. (3) "The same number" holds on a store whose indexes agree with their table. An index missing entries reads short (the independent read built one: 400 against 450, `count_status` ok), and `PRAGMA quick_check`, which `src/database/verify.py` runs, does not see that either; only `integrity_check` does. The old statement also read every article row, so a damaged `articles` page surfaced here as an `error`; the new one reads one index and does not, while damage to a page of that index now shows as an `error` in the FTS block though the full-text index is fine. The foreign-key check and `integrity_check` read the table. (4) The two other counts in this path are bare `count(*)` and uninterruptible: step 4's `(SELECT count(*) FROM article_fts_docsize)` (bounded by the shadow table's size, one short row per article, 61 to 82 ms cold at 1 M; #1534's "one bounded block" is true of the block and not of that statement, and its comment now says so) and `monitoring/integrity.py`'s own `SELECT count(*) FROM articles` (0.2 to 0.3 s cold). Rewriting them to `count(1)` is a one-token change to #1534's accepted statement text and to a second file, ruled R299 a stated limit and a line in the P2 proposal, not a PR. (5) `integrity.py` takes its own count of `articles` beside the status's, in two snapshots, so under a live ingest the report's `articles` and `fts_rows` can differ; harmless (`delta` uses `articles` only), 0.2 to 0.3 s cold, and reusing `rows` for it is a possible follow-on. (6) `PRAGMA foreign_key_check` (`src/monitoring/integrity.py:217`, inside the shared 60 s `statement_deadline` of `corpus_integrity`) is now about 90% of the integrity report cold at this size (11 to 15 s), on a store whose keyword tables are EMPTY: a real store's cost is larger and unmeasured. It is a proposal of its own (`wal-lane/proposal-fk-check-deadline-2026-10-27.md`) and was not touched. (7) The boot still compares no counts: a decision, left as it was; whether it may now is P2, a proposal. (8) `rows` is still the content table's count and not the index's size; the integrity `delta` and `fts.index_state` carry the index. (9) The measurement stores were deleted after the figures were recorded; the scripts and raw results are in `wal-lane/m1m-data/` (`results_p1.jsonl` for the first version, `results_p1b.jsonl` for the final statement).
