## 2026-10-16 — the storage-events journal keeps the longest events as well as the newest, and each kept event says which it is (PR #1421)

The cap of four kinds is split in `storage_events.LONGEST`: `gate_hold` keeps its 10 longest by `seconds` and the 10 newest, `guard` 5 and 5 by `seconds`, `pool_timeout` 5 and 5 by `waited_s`, `open` 5 and 5 by `total_ms`. `_select` is the one function every cut goes through (record, the read of the file, the combine of file and process): the longest and the newest are both kept, a repeat of both is counted once and the room it leaves goes to the next newest, ties go to the newer, and a bool, a string, a NaN or a missing field is never a length. `picked_by` (`longest`, `newest`, `both`) rides on each kept event of those kinds and `keep_rule` names the split in the member; the budget cut drops the oldest of the others first and a longest event last. Totals and dropped counts are unchanged, an event that is cut is released from the open table, and the slot's measured widths are 73,714 bytes with ordinary names and 118,424 with every name at the cut (the rule adds 1,494) against the slot of 125,000.

The case from the 16 Oct bundles is the first test: one 219.6 s hold, then sixty of 15 s, and the long one is still there tagged `longest` with 41 counted as dropped. 29 tests; 23 mutants of the rule each killed by a named test (two survived the first set of tests, a bool and a NaN read as a length, and brought the NaN-among-real-lengths test on the function by itself).
