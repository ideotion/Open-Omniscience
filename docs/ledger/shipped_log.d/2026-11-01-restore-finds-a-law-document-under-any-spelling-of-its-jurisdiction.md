## 2026-11-01 — a restore finds a law document under any spelling of its jurisdiction, so an old backup over a new paste is one row (R403 / R399; PR #1682)

**What was found.** PR #1679 made `POST /api/law/documents` store the catalogue's own spelling of a typed jurisdiction. Its independent read measured the other side, through the real `merge_corpus`: a backup written by the OLD route holds the spelling that route stored (`gb`, `GBR`, `euu`, `ant`, `uae`, `drc`), the restore compared the two documents by the EXACT text of (jurisdiction, url), and the staged copy's conversion toward the store's form (`to_iso2`/`to_iso3`) refuses the app's own codes. So restored over a store whose document was pasted after #1679 with the same alias typed on both sides, it added a second row where it used to find one. Over every pair of two- and three-letter spellings of one jurisdiction: 6 pairs (alpha-2 store) and 8 pairs (alpha-3 store, every new install) went from one row to two, the UK typed `gb` or `gbr` among them, against 506 and 8 pairs that went from two rows to one. The coordinator ruled (R403) to ship #1679 with that stated and to close it next; this is that close.

**What changed.** The restore compares a law document's jurisdiction through ONE key on BOTH sides, `law_jurisdiction_key` (`src/backup/country_codes.py`): the catalogue's form for a code of two or three ASCII letters (`FRA`, `fra` and `fr` are `fr`; `GBR`, `gb` and `uk` are `uk`; `EUU` is `eu`), the string exactly as stored for anything else (a word such as `france`, `georgia` or `congo` is never guessed into a code, the same rule as the paste route). `_merge_law` (`src/backup/merge.py`) keys every law document of the working copy and of the staged copy into two indexed temp tables (once before the match, and the working copy again after the insert so the just-inserted documents map too) and the duplicate count, the INSERT's `NOT EXISTS` and the `map_law` all join those instead of the text, except for a SIBLING (a staged document whose identity another staged document shares: the backup itself holds one document under two spellings), which is matched by its exact spelling as it always was, so no two staged documents are ever mapped onto one working-copy row. Nothing is rewritten: the INSERT still copies `i.jurisdiction` as staged. A staged document maps to the working copy's row for the same key; where the working copy holds the key under two spellings (a row the old route stored beside the catalogue's, which the unique index lets sit side by side) the row spelled like the staged document is the home, else the oldest, which is the row an exact match found whenever one existed. The two places that find the row an ALTERNATE differs from by its recorded jurisdiction (`_alternate_local_match` in the merge, `_local_row_id` in `alternates.py`) read both sides through the same key and choose the document by the same rule as the map: the one spelled exactly as the identity says, else the oldest. The paste route (`src/api/law.py`) now takes the code shape and the key from the same module, so the writer and the reader share one definition.

**Pinned by** `tests/test_restore_law_jurisdiction_canonical.py` (282 tests, 127 red on the unchanged merge and route): every pair of stored spellings of one jurisdiction across the four combinations of source and target store form is one row with the target untouched and one duplicate counted; the old-backup-over-a-new-paste case by name for the UK and the four other aliases, and the other direction; two jurisdictions stay two rows and a word is never guessed into a code; the revisions of a matched document land on the working copy's row and a new document brings its own; where the working copy holds two spellings the exactly-spelled one, else the oldest, is the home; a backup holding two spellings of one document is restored as it always was, over an empty copy AND over a copy that already holds the document (with the shared revision and the two summaries each staying on its own document), restoring it again finds both, and no combination of staged and held spellings maps two staged documents onto one row; the merge's placing of an alternate and the app's later lookup resolve to the same summary (exact spelling first, else the oldest document); and the key itself. 42 mutants, each run after a passing baseline on a harness that writes no bytecode and compares a snapshot after every run, are all killed. `tests/test_country_code_normaliser.py::test_flip_day_is_REPORTED...` kept its measurement and changed its last assertion: it used a law document restored as two rows to show the form gap was not theatre, and that table no longer shows the consequence. The independent read of the first version found three defects, each reproduced and now fixed and pinned: a backup holding one document under two spellings with a revision of the same text on each failed the whole restore on `UNIQUE (document_id, content_hash)` when the working copy already held the document; two summaries by one model could land on one revision; and the app and the merge chose different documents for an alternate.

**Cost, measured** (50,000 law documents on each side, an in-memory Python key per row, the sibling flag included): keying 0.39 s for the first side (it imports the catalogue) and 0.13 s for the second, the duplicate count 0.04 s, the map 0.07 s; both branches of the match use an index (`SEARCH km USING COVERING INDEX law_key_main_key_url (key=? AND url=?)` and `SEARCH m USING COVERING INDEX ix_lawdoc_jurisdiction_url`). The catalogue holds thousands of documents, so the restore pays well under a second.

**Stated limits.** Rows already stored in another spelling stay as they are (the restore does not rewrite the working copy; a cleanup is the country migration's job), and a new row keeps the spelling it was staged in. A jurisdiction typed as a WORD on one install and as a code on another (`france` and `fr`) is still two rows, as before: a word is never guessed. A backup that itself holds two spellings of one document is restored as two rows, as before (each spelling matched exactly). An alternate's recorded spelling is converted to the store's form before it is compared, as it always was (unchanged by this change, found by its delta read): where the working copy holds one document under its store-form spelling AND under another (`gbr` and `gb` in an alpha-2 store, `gb` and `GBR` in an alpha-3 store), an alternate captured from the first can be shown or swapped against the second; preferring the recorded spelling first, then the store form, is the follow-on. The duplicate-key scan diagnostic still lists the app's own codes (`uk`, `eu`, `int`) as unresolved: it is read-only and was not changed. The identity JSON that `_capture_alternates` compares as text was not examined for the same two-spellings case. Not run against a real backup of a real install.
