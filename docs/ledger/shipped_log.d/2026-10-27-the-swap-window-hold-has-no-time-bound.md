## 2026-10-27 — `swap_window`'s docstring now says the hold has no time bound and why, what IS bounded, and how long the one data-dependent term (the last close's write-back inside `dispose_engine()`) measured (coordinator item 3 of 18:39Z, ruling R326 option A; comment-only; A-029 unchanged)

**The question (A-029, R306 (1), R311).** Is the swap window's hold bounded, and should it be? `WINDOW_WAIT_S` = 60 s bounds only the wait; the restore and repair file steps share `_SWAP_HANDLE_WAIT_S` = 300 s; the encrypt body (`disk_changing`, two unlinks, one `os.replace`) has no budget; connection opens behind the window wait in steps of `_RECHECK_S` = 1 s for as long as it is held.

**Ruling and change.** R326: no time bound on the hold; the code says so. The body runs on its caller's thread inside calls nothing can cancel, giving the window back while it runs is the hazard it exists to prevent, and failing waiting opens early would turn every request during a legitimate long hold into an error on an unmeasured number. `src/database/library_generation.py`, `swap_window`'s docstring only (no executable line): what is bounded, what is not, why no timer, the measured hold, the field figure as not reproduced, the fold before the window as unmeasured, the R199 release call as the one hold the code can leave unbounded (loud through the pool's waits and `pool_timeout`; a detector is a proposal).

**The measurement (the lesson, `lessons.d/2026-10-27-the-swap-window-hold-has-no-time-bound.md`, has the table).** RC2's `dispose_engine()` at 0.0 to 0.001 s had no log to write back (the generator store is in rollback-journal mode). With a leftover log of N MiB on the pool's one connection (the last on the file), the app's own engine, a 4-core virtual disk, one or two cells each: 0.001 s with no log, 0.88 s plain and 0.98 s encrypted at 100 MiB, 0.7 to 2.6 s at 300 MiB, 5.6 to 8.1 s at 1,000 MiB, warm and cold. Plain: other threads run and a connection open waits for the end of the window. Encrypted: the whole process stands still (the ticker's worst gap equals the dispose in every cell; `sqlcipher3`'s `close()` keeps the GIL). The field's 90.8 s for a 1.41 GB log at one unlock is NOT reproduced (a different disk, about 65 ms a MiB against 2 to 10 here); nothing is extrapolated. Restore and repair fold the log before the window and refuse when they cannot, so the window should find only what was written between the fold and the window; how many frames that is in a real restore is not measured.

**Options named, none built:** a timeout in `connecting()`; recording the hold; a stuck-hold detector (the settings lock's `_refuse_if_stuck` is the precedent); disposing before the window in `swap_in_corpus`. Raw rows and the probe: the WAL lane's `swap-window-hold-data/` and `proposal-swap-window-hold-bound-2026-10-27.md`, outside the repository.
