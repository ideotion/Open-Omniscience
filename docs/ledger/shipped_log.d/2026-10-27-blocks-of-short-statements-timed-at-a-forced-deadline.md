## 2026-10-27 — `statement_deadline`'s docstring now says what it does not bound, with the measured size of that gap warm on a plain store and cold on a 9.9 M-mention encrypted one; no block in `src/` is held open by many distinct short statements (coordinator item 1 of 15:27Z, R321; comment-only, with the Library mentions count re-measure, item 2, as the second record in the bundle)

**The question (R299 follow-on to #1543 and #1563).** The progress handler's 20,000-opcode counter belongs to one prepared statement, so a block of many DISTINCT short statements runs whole under any deadline. Is any block in `src/` such a block at a size that matters?

**What was done (read and measure only; the only edit is a comment).** Every `statement_deadline` was forced to a millionth of a second by a wrapper installed before the app imports (a block asking for 0, disabled by design, left alone), and the time each block stayed open was recorded, split into SQL time, the longest single call, Python, the statement count and the distinct text count, for 327 parameterless GET routes (50 entered a block), 73 parameterised routes (42 entered a block), the 57 members of the diagnostics bundle (32 touch `db`), and two direct drives (`build_stories`, `content_share_estimate`). Stores: the app's schema, synthetic. Plain SQLite 3.45.1, warm, 60,000 articles / 169,695 mentions (163 MiB) and 300,000 / 848,841 (794 MiB); and SQLCipher 4.12.0 on SQLite 3.51.1, **cold** (file evicted with `posix_fadvise(DONTNEED)` plus a fresh process per walk), 300,000 articles / 9,900,000 mentions (3,220 MiB), through the app's own engine.

**Result.** No block is held open by the class. The distinct-text count per block is fixed by the code and the schema (identical at 60,000 and 300,000 articles in 77 of 81 cases; a search for SQL that inlines a data value found none in a deadline block); the largest is `schema-drift.json`, **285 texts** (63 tables + 182 indexes), **0.25 s** warm plain and **0.449 s** cold encrypted. The 32 database-touching bundle members each returned within **0.87 s** warm plain and **0.92 s** cold encrypted (13 `skipped-deadline`, 19 `partial-deadline`), against up to **14.1 s** as shipped (`performance.json`). Reaching the 60 s deadline would take about 660,000 distinct statements in one block (about 90 microseconds each).

**What changed (`src/database/maintenance.py`, comment only).** The `statement_deadline` docstring gains two paragraphs: WHAT IT DOES NOT BOUND (statements that each end under 20,000 opcodes, a one-opcode statement such as a bare `count(*)` or the aggregated `dbstat`, Python between statements; the handler is armed on the session's connection only; a loop asks `deadline_expired`) and HOW MUCH (the figures above, each labelled by store and size, pointing to the lesson). No behaviour changed and no per-statement expiry hook was added (a measured gap of 0.25 s does not pay for a change to the file the whole app runs through; a proposal if a block ever shows more than a few thousand distinct statements).

**Named limits, no PR (R299; the table is in `lessons.d/2026-10-27-blocks-of-short-statements-timed-at-a-forced-deadline.md`).** The simplemma first-use load, 6.6 s warm plain and 6.13 s cold encrypted, inside `GET /api/insights/associations` (Python, once per process, routed to the keyword lane); the keyword export blocks, deadline 0 by design, **38.6 s and 76.3 s** open at 9.9 M mentions cold (5.1 s and 9.5 s at 0.85 M warm); the `minhash` stretch of `corpus-coordination` (1.0 to 1.3 s warm, 400 documents); `dbstat` in `storage.json` (0.85 s, plain only); the stories YAML parse (0.6 s); the app-state KV store's own connection in three bundle members (0.31 to 0.56 s cold, against 0.007 to 0.026 s warm), the keyword export's read snapshot, and the bundle members that touch no `db` (`selftests.json` 8.49 s, `merge-diag.json` 7.28 s cold); the framing route and seven `_deadlined` callers not driven; the DuckDB rollup builds, invisible to the recording.
