## 2026-10-16 — one extraction at a time, process-wide: a lock around the whole `extract_article` call tree (PR #1424, provisional until the PR is opened)

The export lane's follow-up to the 2026-10-14 SIGABRT in `lxml/html/__init__.py:740` (the coordinator's conditions of 2026-10-16 20:32Z: measure what the lock costs and state it in the PR, register any trafilatura bound in the same commit, name the evidence as inferred, and add an OPEN_QUEUE item for subprocess isolation). `src/ingest/extract.py` gained `_LXML_LOCK`, a `threading.RLock` around the whole of `extract_article` (the body moved to `_extract_locked`; a blank page still returns before the lock is asked for). Every parse, tree walk and tree release of the app's HTML happens inside that call tree, so concurrent use of libxml2 is impossible from the app whatever the fault was; the result holds text only, so on a return the trees are freed inside the lock, and on a raise `_release_frames` clears the frames of the exception and its `__cause__`/`__context__` chain while the lock is still held (a traceback keeps its frames, and their trees, alive past the `with`).

`tests/test_extract_lock.py` (55 tests): both paths (the one-parse path and the two-call fallback) never overlap across 8 and 6 threads, the lock is released when an extraction raises (asked from ANOTHER thread, since an RLock would let the leaking thread back in), a nested call does not deadlock (a plain `Lock` is refused by an RLock-type check before the nesting call, and an autouse fixture asks for the lock with a one-second timeout so a lock left held by a deadlocked or leaked holder fails the next test instead of hanging the run), a blank page does not wait for it, and the trees are freed while it is held, on a return and on a raise (a sentinel `__del__` asks `_is_owned()`; the raise test also checks that the traceback still names the frames), plus an AST walk of all of `src/` for every way into libxml2 and of `extract.py` for calls to trafilatura outside the locked functions, each proved on fixture sources. The walks do not see a dynamic import by a computed name, or a library reached through another package's C extension; the test docstring says so. Nine mutants of `extract.py`, all killed.

Cost, measured on 4 CPUs (trafilatura 2.2.0, lxml 6.1.3, libxml2 2.14.6; `collect_throughput_bench.py threads`, a new mode, and `sweep`, unchanged): extraction alone, 24 pages of about 22,000 characters, best of 3, articles per second at 1 / 4 / 16 / 50 threads, unlocked 213.6 / 57.1 / 52.7 / 46.1, locked 231.6 / 218.1 / 221.0 / 209.4; 12 pages of about 120,000 characters at 1 / 8 / 50 threads, unlocked 55.8 / 12.2 / 10.9, locked 52.2 / 51.3 / 45.2. Whole pass (96 articles stored every run), 1 / 8 / 50 workers: fetch delay 0.02 s unlocked 3.85 / 5.35 / 5.19, locked 4.21 / 6.19 / 6.17; delay 1.5 s unlocked 0.51 / 2.40 / 3.77, locked 0.51 / 2.45 / 3.92. Again on trafilatura 2.3.1 (htmldate 1.11.0), 24 pages of about 22,000 characters at 1 / 4 / 16 / 50 threads: unlocked 262.5 / 96.8 / 101.1 / 98.4, locked 276.3 / 320.4 / 291.1 / 267.2. Unlocked threaded extraction is 3.8 to 4.5 times slower than locked from 4 threads up on 2.2.0 and 2.7 to 3.3 times on 2.3.1, and about 10% is noise (a small reviewer sample showed the locked run 13% slower at one thread); the cause is not profiled (a guess: GIL and allocator contention). THE TAIL, the cost the lock does have: one long page holds the lock for every extraction, and nothing times it out. A synthetic nav-heavy page took 6.2 s at 1 MB, 34.7 s at 5 MB and 85.3 s at 10 MB (paragraph-only 0.5 / 3.5 / 9.4 s); reproduced at 1 MB (5.1 s on 2.2.0, 4.6 s on 2.3.1); a small page behind a 1.2 MB page took 6.18 s locked and 0.76 s unlocked, and the fetcher admits up to 10 MiB. Shipped with that limit documented; subprocess isolation is the recorded next step. A gc probe (48 extractions, `DEBUG_SAVEALL`, extended by the reviewer to the metadata-fault and body-fault paths) left zero unreachable objects. `gc.collect` and `malloc_trim` also run from `memguard`/`memstop` during a pass and `reset_caches` runs in the pause loop, so nothing excludes them from a running pool by construction.

At the coordinator's request after the tail was measured (2026-10-16 22:01Z): `src/ingest/lock_stats.py` records, per monitored collect pass, how many extractions held the lock (`holds`, whatever each returned), the longest and total wait and hold, and the UTF-8 length of the text handed to `extract_article` for the longest hold. `CollectionMonitor` opens the window at `start` and writes the snapshot into the pass's summary line in `collect_perf.jsonl` as `extraction_lock`, and `run_scrape_once` copies the same block into the pass result, which is the run report in `scheduler_runs.jsonl` and so `scheduler.recent_runs` (30 runs, slim copy included) in the debug bundle: no new bundle member. The slim copy keeps only the newest 20 lines of the perf ring (about 30 s of a running pass), which is why the run report carries it too. Numbers only, no URL or text; a pass with no hold says `holds: 0`; an unmeasurable page says so; an unreadable statistic says the exception class. The record is taken after the lock is given up, and the longest hold's page is measured once, outside the private lock, from that hold's own text. `tests/test_extract_lock_stats.py` (25 tests, on a fake clock; 29 mutants, 28 killed and one equivalent) pins each. Found by the independent read of this commit: a two-step update that could pair a size with the wrong hold under a race, a reset or a failed measurement (reproduced, then made a single atomic update), a false claim about the slim ring, and `extractions` counting holds that returned nothing (renamed `holds`). Limits: the figures are written when the pass ends, a hold that ends while no pass runs is in no pass's window, and a pass without a monitor writes no block.
