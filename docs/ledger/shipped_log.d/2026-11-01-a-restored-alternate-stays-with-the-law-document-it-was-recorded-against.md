## 2026-11-01 — a restored alternate stays with the law document it was recorded against, and a carried alternate is stored once per local document (R403 follow-on; PR #1683)

**What was found.** PR #1682 made a restore find a law document under any spelling of its jurisdiction and left one limit in its notes: an alternate's recorded spelling is converted to the store's form before it is compared. A backup holding ONE document under two spellings (`gbr` and `gb` in an alpha-2 store; the restore keeps both rows) carried an alternate against `gbr`; the staged conversion turned its identity into `gb`, so the alternate was placed on, and later shown against, the other document. The duplicate check on carried alternates compared the identity TEXT, which is the wrong unit once one identity can name two documents.

**What changed.** (1) `convert_alternate_countries(..., keep_with_unconverted_document=False)` (`src/backup/country_codes.py`): the restore passes True (`normalise_staged_country_codes`), and a law identity whose exact spelling and `document_url` the staged `law_documents` still holds is left as recorded ("identity follows its document"); the migration keeps converting; the report gains `kept_with_document`, only when nonzero. (2) One placement order in the restore (`_alternate_local_match`, `src/backup/merge.py`) and the app (`alternates._local_row_id`): the recorded spelling, then the store's form, then the oldest document; a padded recorded spelling is a candidate; a non-text or empty jurisdiction counts as absent and never raises. (3) For the law scope, a carried alternate is a duplicate when `(origin, fields_json, local row)` is already held, the held set read once per restore and extended after each insert (`_merge_metadata_alternates`), and the capture (`_capture_alternates`) compares on the row too, through an indexed temporary table dropped in a `finally`; the identity-text check stays for every scope and the non-law SQL is byte-identical. Consequence: one record on two documents is stored once per document, and the order the backups are restored in does not matter.

**Pinned by** `tests/test_restore_law_jurisdiction_canonical.py` (349 tests; 495 across the seven alternate, country and restore files on SQLite 3.45.1 and 3.53.1; 4,580 guard tests on the rebased tree) and 38 mutants of the conversion, both placement orderings, the row check, the read-once set and the capture check, all killed. Timing: 10,000 captured against 10,000 carried alternates, 0.61 s against the parent's 0.59 s.

**Stated limits.** The same backup restored before AND after the upgrade stores the alternate twice (the legacy copy on `gb`, the new one on `gbr`; it needs a backup holding siblings). A legacy identity with no jurisdiction, or a non-text one, over siblings: the restore picks the oldest revision, the app the newest summary (unchanged). A stale `local_row_id` after a reused rowid could read as a duplicate; no code path deletes law rows today. Not run against a real backup of a real install.
